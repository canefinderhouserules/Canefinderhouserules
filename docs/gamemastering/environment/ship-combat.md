# Fast-Play Ship Combat

A quick and easy set of rules for resolving ship-to-ship combat, or a battle between a ship and a sea monster. They are not meant to simulate all the complexities of naval combat. Combat between creatures aboard a ship functions almost identically to combat on land. If it happens during a storm or in heavy seas, treat the ship's deck as difficult terrain, and take into account the effects on spellcasters' concentration checks due to weather or the motion of the deck.

## Aquatic Adaptation

The Core Rulebook underwater combat rules apply to creatures not native to the environment. For extended aquatic adventures or deep explorations, PCs will need magic: *water breathing* to breathe, *endure elements* for temperature, and effects such as *freedom of movement* to avoid pressure damage. Polymorph spells are particularly useful if the form assumed is aquatic.

**Natural Adaptation**: Any creature with the aquatic subtype can breathe water easily and is unaffected by water temperature extremes in its typical environment. Aquatic creatures and creatures with the hold breath ability are much more resistant to pressure damage; they take none unless moved instantaneously from one depth to another, in which case they adapt after making five successive Fortitude saves against the pressure effects.

## Running Ship Combat

**Preparation**: Decide what type of ships are involved (see the table below). Use a large, blank battle mat to represent the waters. A single square corresponds to 30 feet. Represent each ship by markers that take up the appropriate number of squares.

**Starting Combat**: Allow the PCs (and important NPC allies) to roll initiative as normal. The ship itself moves and attacks on the captain's initiative result. If any ship relies on sails, randomly determine the wind direction by rolling 1d8 and following the guidelines for missed splash weapons.

**Movement**: On the captain's initiative count, the ship can move its current speed in a single round as a move action for the captain (or double its speed as a full-round action), as long as it has its minimum crew complement. The ship can increase or decrease its speed by 30 feet each round, up to its maximum speed. Alternatively, the captain can change direction (up to one side of a square at a time) as a standard action. A ship can only change direction at the start of a turn.

**Attacks**: Crewmembers in excess of the minimum crew requirement can be allocated to man siege engines, which attack on the captain's initiative count. A ship with its minimum crew can also attempt to ram a target. It must move at least 30 feet and end with its bow in a square adjacent to the target. The captain makes a Profession (sailor) check; if it equals or exceeds the target's AC, the ship hits, inflicting damage as indicated on the table to the target, as well as minimum damage to the ramming ship. A ship outfitted with an actual ram siege engine inflicts an additional 3d6 points of damage to the target (the ramming vessel suffers no additional damage).

## Sinking

A ship gains the sinking condition if its hit points are reduced to 0 or fewer. A sinking ship cannot move or attack, and sinks completely 10 rounds after it gains the condition. Each hit on a sinking ship that inflicts damage reduces the remaining time to sink by 1 round per 25 points of damage. Magic (such as *make whole*) can repair a sinking ship if its hit points are raised above 0, at which point it loses the condition. Generally, nonmagical repairs take too long to save a ship once it begins to go down.

- **AC**: The ship's base Armor Class. To calculate its actual AC, add the captain's Profession (sailor) modifier. Touch attacks against a ship ignore the captain's modifier. A ship is never considered flat-footed.
- **hp**: The ship's total hit points. All ships have a hardness rating based on their construction material (hardness 5 for most wooden ships).
- **Base Save**: All of a ship's saving throws (Fortitude, Reflex, and Will) have the same value. Add the captain's Profession (sailor) modifier to this base value.
- **Maximum Speed**: The ship's maximum tactical speed in combat. An asterisk indicates the ship has sails and can move at double speed when moving in the same direction as the wind. A ship with only sails can only move if there is some wind.
- **Arms**: The number of siege engines that can be fitted on the ship. A ram uses one of these slots, and only one ram may be fitted to a ship.
- **Ram**: The damage the ship inflicts on a successful ramming attack (without a ram siege engine).
- **Squares**: The number of squares the ship takes up on the battle mat. A ship's width is always one square.
- **Crew**: The first number is the minimum crew the ship needs to function normally, excluding those needed to use the vessel's weapons. The second is the maximum crew plus additional soldiers or passengers. A ship without its minimum crew can only move, change speed, change direction, or ram if its captain makes a DC 20 Profession (sailor) check. Crew in excess of the minimum have no effect on movement, but they can replace fallen crewmembers or man additional weapons.

## Plunder

When a party takes a ship, record its cargo as points of plunder. As a general rule, 1 point of plunder is worth about 1,000 gp and takes up about 1 ton of cargo capacity. Tracking a ship's value as plunder points (rather than raw treasure) helps a GM keep player characters from obtaining too much gold too quickly, since a vessel's price, cargo, and crew gear can all but guarantee deviation from the expected wealth by level.

## Nautical Weapons

Seafaring weapons such as cutlasses and boarding pikes differ in description from other weapons but not in statistics: a cutlass is statistically identical to a scimitar, and a boarding pike is statistically identical to a longspear.
