# Settlements

Optional rules for describing the towns and cities the PCs visit with a stat block, six skill-affecting modifiers, qualities, and disadvantages. Introducing settlement modifiers adds a variable modifier each time the PCs visit a new town, so consider them an optional rule.

## The Settlement Stat Block

- **Alignment and Type**: A settlement's alignment is the general alignment of its citizens and government; individuals can be of any alignment, but the majority should be within one step of the settlement's overall alignment. Alignment influences modifiers. The type is the size category: thorp, hamlet, village, town (small or large), city (small or large), or metropolis. A settlement's type determines many of its statistics (see the table below).
- **Modifiers**: Settlements possess six modifiers that apply to specific skill checks made in the settlement. Starting values are determined by type and further adjusted by alignment, government, qualities, and disadvantages.
- **Qualities**: Like feats for settlements. A settlement's type determines how many qualities it can have.
- **Danger**: A number that gives a general idea of how dangerous it is to live in the settlement. If you use a random encounter chart for your city that ranks encounters from lowest to highest CR, use the danger value to adjust rolls on the chart.
- **Disadvantages**: Any disadvantages a settlement suffers from. A settlement can have any number, though most have none.
- **Government**: How the settlement is governed. It affects the statistics.
- **Population**: The average population, with a breakdown of its racial mix. This number is generally used for little more than flavor, since actual totals fluctuate.
- **Notable NPCs**: Notable NPCs, sorted by their role in the community.
- **Base Value and Purchase Limit**: The community's base value for available magic items in gp. There is a 75% chance that any item of this value or lower can be found for sale with little effort. If an item is not available, a new check can be made in 1 week. The purchase limit is the most money a shop in the settlement can spend to purchase any single item from the PCs. If the PCs wish to sell an item worth more, they must settle for a lower price, travel to a larger city, or (with the GM's permission) search for a specific buyer with deeper pockets.
- **Spellcasting**: The highest-level spell available for purchase from spellcasters in town.
- **Minor Items / Medium Items / Major Items**: The number of magic items above the settlement's base value that are available for purchase (see Table 7–37).

## Settlement Modifiers

Life in a settlement is represented by six modifiers, each of which adjusts the use of specific skills there.

**Corruption**: How open a settlement's officials are to bribes, how honest its citizens are, and how likely anyone is to report a crime. It modifies all Bluff checks made against city officials or guards and all Stealth checks made outside (but not inside buildings or underground).

**Crime**: A measure of lawlessness. A low value means violent crimes are rare; a high value means a powerful thieves' guild and a significant problem with violence. It applies as a modifier on Sense Motive checks to avoid being bluffed and to Sleight of Hand checks made to pick pockets.

**Economy**: The health of trade and the wealth of successful citizens. A low value doesn't automatically mean poverty; it could be a town with little trade or one that is relatively self-sufficient. It applies as a modifier on all Craft, Perform, and Profession checks made to generate income.

**Law**: How strict the laws and edicts are. A high modifier means guards are alert, vigilant, and well organized, and the more lawful a town is, the more timidly its citizens respond to shows of force. It applies on Intimidate checks made to force an opponent to act friendly, Diplomacy checks against government officials, and Diplomacy checks made to call on the city guard.

**Lore**: How willing citizens are to chat with visitors, and how available and accessible libraries and sages are. It applies on Diplomacy checks made to gather information and Knowledge checks made using the city's resources to do research in a library.

**Society**: How open-minded and civilized the citizens are. A low value might mean many citizens harbor prejudices or are overly suspicious of out-of-towners; a high value means citizens are used to diversity and respond better to well-spoken attempts at conversation. It applies on all Disguise checks, as well as Diplomacy checks made to alter the attitude of any non-government official.

### Calling the Guards

It is a DC 5 Diplomacy check, modified by the settlement's law modifier, to call for the guard. With a success, the guards generally arrive in 1d6 minutes. Every 5 points by which the check exceeds DC 5 (rounding down) reduces the arrival time by 1 minute; if this reduces the arrival time below 1 minute, the increments of reduction instead change to 1 round.

### Settlement Alignment

A settlement's alignment influences its modifiers. A lawful component increases its law modifier by 1. A good component increases its society modifier by 1. A chaotic component increases its crime modifier by 1. An evil component increases its corruption modifier by 1. A neutral component increases its lore modifier by 1 (a truly neutral city gains an increase of 2 to lore). Alignment never modifies a settlement's economy modifier.

## Settlement Government

Choose one of the following. A government adjusts the modifiers.

- **Autocracy**: A single individual chosen by the people rules the community (mayor, burgomaster, lord, or a royal title). No modifiers.
- **Council**: A group of councilors, often guild masters or aristocrats, leads the settlement. Society +4; Law and Lore –2.
- **Magical**: An individual or group with potent magical power, such as a high priest, an archwizard, or a magical monster, leads the community. Lore +2; Corruption and Society –2; increase spellcasting by 1 level.
- **Overlord**: A single individual who seized control or inherited command. Corruption and Law +2; Crime and Society –2.
- **Secret Syndicate**: An unofficial or illegal group like a thieves' guild rules the settlement, perhaps through a puppet leader. Corruption, Economy, and Crime +2; Law –6.

## Settlement Qualities

A settlement's type determines how many qualities it can have; once a quality is chosen, it cannot be changed. Where qualities adjust base value or purchase limit by a percentage, add together the percentages and then increase the value by the aggregated total; do not apply the increases one at a time.

- **Academic**: A school, training facility, or university of great renown. Lore +1; increase spellcasting by 1 level.
- **Holy Site**: A shrine, temple, or landmark with great significance to one or more religions, with a higher percentage of divine spellcasters. Corruption –2; increase spellcasting by 2 levels.
- **Insular**: Isolated, physically or spiritually; citizens are fiercely loyal to one another. Law +1; Crime –1.
- **Magically Attuned**: A haven for spellcasters due to its location, such as a convergence of ley lines or a well-known magical site. Increase base value by 20%; increase purchase limit by 20%; increase spellcasting by 2 levels.
- **Notorious**: A reputation (deserved or not) as a den of iniquity. Crime +1; Law –1; Danger +10; increase base value by 30%; increase purchase limit by 50%.
- **Pious**: Known for good manners, a friendly spirit, and deep devotion to a deity of the same alignment as the community. Increase spellcasting by 1 level. Any faith more than one alignment step different from the community's official religion is at best unwelcome and at worst outlawed; obvious worshipers of an outlawed deity pay 150% of the normal price for goods and services and may face mockery, insult, or even violence.
- **Prosperous**: A popular hub for trade; merchants are wealthy and citizens live well. Economy +1; increase base value by 30%; increase purchase limit by 50%.
- **Racially Intolerant**: Prejudiced against one or more listed races. Members of the unwelcome races pay 150% of the normal price for goods and services and may face mockery, insult, or even violence.
- **Rumormongering Citizens**: Nosy and gossipy to a fault. Lore +1; Society –1.
- **Strategic Location**: At an important crossroads or deepwater port, or serving as a barrier to a pass or bridge. Economy +1; increase base value by 10%.
- **Superstitious**: A deep fear of magic and the unexplained, which makes citizens more supportive and loyal to each other. Crime –4; Law and Society +2; reduce spellcasting by 2 levels.
- **Tourist Attraction**: A landmark or event that draws visitors from far and wide. Economy +1; increase base value by 20%.

## Settlement Disadvantages

There is no limit to the number of disadvantages a community can suffer, but most have none, since a settlement plagued by disadvantages for too long eventually collapses. A disadvantage can arise as the result of an event or an action by a powerful NPC or PC. By going on a quest or accomplishing a noteworthy deed, heroes can remove a disadvantage.

- **Anarchy**: No leaders; such a community is often short-lived and dangerous. Replaces the settlement's government and removes government adjustments to modifiers; Corruption and Crime +4; Economy and Society –4; Law –6; Danger +20.
- **Cursed**: Some form of curse afflicts the city: violence-prone citizens, ill luck, or an infestation of pests. Choose one modifier and reduce its value by 4.
- **Hunted**: A powerful group or monster uses the city as its hunting ground, and citizens avoid going out unless necessary. Economy, Law, and Society –4; Danger +20; reduce base value by 20%.
- **Impoverished**: The settlement is destitute, with poverty, famine, and disease rampant. Corruption and Crime +1; decrease base value and purchase limit by 50%; halve magic item availability.
- **Plagued**: Suffering from a protracted contagion or malady. –2 to all modifiers; reduce base value by 20%; select a communicable disease: there is a 5% chance each day that a PC is exposed and must make a Fortitude save to avoid contracting it.
