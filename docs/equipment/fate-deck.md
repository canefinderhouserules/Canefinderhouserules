# Fate Deck

A fate deck is a tool used by fortune-tellers to divine the future, to gamble, and, in the hands of the inventive, to fight, steal and summon. It consists of 54 cards divided into six suits: **books**, **crowns**, **hammers**, **keys**, **shields**, and **stars**. Each suit contains nine cards, one for each alignment, and each suit is linked to one of the six ability scores.

| Suit | Ability Score |
|---|---|
| Hammers | Strength |
| Keys | Dexterity |
| Shields | Constitution |
| Books | Intelligence |
| Stars | Wisdom |
| Crowns | Charisma |

Several class options in the wiki (the [fate warden](../characters/classes/monk-archetypes.md), [cartomancer](../characters/classes/witch-archetypes.md), [card caster](../characters/classes/magus-archetypes.md), [card swindler](../characters/classes/rogue-archetypes.md), [story summoner](../characters/classes/summoner-archetypes.md), [suit seeker](../characters/classes/inquisitor-archetypes.md), and the [fatebound sorcerer bloodline](../characters/classes/sorcerer.md)) use a fate deck. The [Deadly Dealer](../rules/feats/deadly-dealer.md) and [Fate-Deck Summoning](../rules/feats/fate-deck-summoning.md) feats also do.

## Using Dice Instead of a Deck

If you don't have a physical deck, roll 1d6 to determine a card's suit (1 = hammers, 2 = keys, 3 = shields, 4 = books, 5 = stars, 6 = crowns) and 1d10 to determine its alignment (1 = LG, 2 = NG, 3 = CG, 4 = LN, 5 = N, 6 = CN, 7 = LE, 8 = NE, 9 = CE, 10 = roll again). You can also use a standard deck of playing cards or a reduced tarot deck, assigning each card a suit and alignment.

## Glossary

**Alignment**: Each suit includes one card for each of the nine alignments.

**Array**: The particular cards laid out or drawn in a reading. The most common arrangement is a three-by-three grid of nine cards. Also called a "spread."

**Misaligned**: A good card in a row associated with evil, an evil card in a row associated with good, or a good or evil card that's reversed. Interpret a misaligned good card in the most negative light, and a misaligned evil card in the most positive light.

**Opposite Match**: Two cards that depict opposite alignments on both axes; for example, a lawful good card is the opposite match of a chaotic evil card.

**Partial Match**: Two cards that depict the same alignment along one axis, but not the other; for example, a lawful good card is the partial match of a neutral good card.

**True Match**: Two cards with the same alignment along both axes; for example, a lawful good card's true match is another lawful good card.

**Suit**: Any of the six sets of nine cards, which also represent the six ability scores.

## Creating Fate Decks

Fate decks can be purchased in shops specializing in curios and arcane goods, but they are rarely cheaper than 100 gp each. Most true fortune-tellers prefer a personal deck, and crafting one requires Craft (cards), Craft (calligraphy), Craft (paintings), or a similar skill. Making a standard fate deck requires a successful DC 15 Craft check and costs 33 gp in raw materials. When a character completes more than 1,000 sp worth of work, the deck is complete.

## Equipment

| Item | Price | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Fate deck carrying case | 10 gp | 2 lbs. |
| Fate mat | 80 gp | 1 lb. |

**Fate Deck Carrying Case** (10 gp, 2 lbs.): This small darkwood case contains a recessed area to hold a fate deck and a fate mat, as well as a compartment for incense. Nonmagical cards are protected against rips, tears, water damage, bending, and flames so long as they are stored in the case.

**Fate Mat** (80 gp, 1 lb.): This thick leather mat is large enough to fit all the cards necessary for a standard reading. If you use a fate mat as part of a reading, you gain a +4 circumstance bonus on Bluff or Diplomacy checks (your choice) to convince the subjects of the reading that your words are true.

## Fortune-Teller Buildings

Players and GMs using the downtime system or kingdom-building rules from Ultimate Campaign can augment the buildings in that book with the following new building types.

**Crooked Fortune-Teller's Shop**: Create 26 Goods, 3 Influence, 24 Labor, 3 Magic (1,390 gp); Rooms 1 False Front, 1 Lavatory, 1 Scrying Room, 1 Secret Room, 1 Storage, 1 Storefront. Unscrupulous card readers dedicate their insight and skill to running simple confidence games, fleecing outsiders, and operating as fences and black marketers.

**Fortune-Teller's Shop**: Create 25 Goods, 4 Influence, 21 Labor, 4 Magic (1,440 gp); Rooms 1 Book Repository (history or religion), 1 Lavatory, 1 Scrying Room, 1 Storage, 1 Storefront. An established fortune-teller offers mystical insight, but makes her day-to-day living performing hedge magic and selling protective amulets and other tokens to superstitious clientele. Masters of many trades, some augment their shops with alchemy labs, artisan's workshops, and infirmaries.
