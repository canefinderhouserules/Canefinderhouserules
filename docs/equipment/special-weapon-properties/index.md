# Special Weapon Properties

<span id="blocking"></span>**Blocking:** When you use this weapon to fight defensively, you gain a +1 shield bonus to AC.

<span id="brace"></span>**Brace:** If you use a readied action to set a brace weapon against a charge, you deal double damage on a successful hit against a charging creature (see Combat).

<span id="deadly"></span>**Deadly:** When you use this weapon to deliver a coup de grace, it gains a +4 bonus to damage.

<span id="disarm"></span>**Disarm:** When you use a disarm weapon, you get a +2 bonus on Combat Maneuver Checks to disarm an enemy.

<span id="distracting"></span>**Distracting:** You gain a +2 bonus on Bluff skill checks to feint in combat while wielding this weapon. Source: PRG:UC.

<span id="double"></span>**Double:** You can use a double weapon to fight as if fighting with two weapons, but if you do, you incur all the normal attack penalties associated with fighting with two weapons, just as if you were using a one-handed weapon and a light weapon. You can choose to wield one end of a double weapon two-handed, but it cannot be used as a double weapon when wielded in this way—only one end of the weapon can be used in any given round.

<span id="fragile"></span>**Fragile:** Weapons and armor with the fragile quality cannot take the beating that sturdier weapons can. A fragile weapon gains the broken condition if the wielder rolls a natural 1 on an attack roll with the weapon. If a fragile weapon is already broken, the roll of a natural 1 destroys it instead. Masterwork and magical fragile weapons and armor lack these flaws unless otherwise noted in the item description or the special material description. If a weapon gains the broken condition in this way, that weapon is considered to have taken damage equal to half its hit points +1. This damage is repaired either by something that addresses the effect that granted the weapon the broken condition (like quick clear in the case of firearm misfires or the Field Repair feat) or by the repair methods described in the broken condition. When an effect that grants the broken condition is removed, the weapon regains the hit points it lost when the broken condition was applied. Damage done by an attack against a weapon (such as from a sunder combat maneuver) cannot be repaired by an effect that removes the broken condition. Source: PRG:UC.

<span id="grapple-crit"></span>**Grapple Crit:** On a successful critical hit with a weapon of this type, you can grapple the target of the attack. The wielder can then attempt a combat maneuver check to grapple his opponent as a free action. This grapple attempt does not provoke an attack of opportunity from the creature you are attempting to grapple if that creature is not threatening you. While you grapple the target with a grappling weapon, you can only move or damage the creature on your turn. You are still considered grappled, though you do not have to be adjacent to the creature to continue the grapple. If you move far enough away to be out of the weapon’s reach, you end the grapple with that action.

<span id="grapple"></span>**Grapple**: Using grapple with this weapon equipped has no penalty, and it even has a +1 bonus on the roll.

<span id="grapple-minus"></span>**Grapple-:** Using grapple with this weapon has no penalty (normally –4).

<span id="monk"></span>**Monk**: A monk weapon can be used by a monk to perform a flurry of blows

<span id="nonlethal"></span>**Nonlethal:** These weapons deal nonlethal damage (see Combat).

<span id="performance"></span>**Performance:** When wielding this weapon, if an attack or combat maneuver made with this weapon prompts a combat performance check, you gain a +2 bonus on that check. See Gladiator Weapons below for more information.

<span id="reach"></span>**Reach:** You use a reach weapon to strike opponents 10 feet away, but you can’t use it against an adjacent foe.

<span id="sunder"></span>**Sunder:** When you use a sunder weapon, you get a +2 bonus on Combat Maneuver Checks to sunder attempts.

<span id="trip"></span>**Trip**: When you use a trip weapon, you get a +2 bonus on Combat Maneuver Checks to trip attempts.

<span id="trip-crit"></span>**Trip Crit**: On a successful critical hit with a weapon of this type, you can trip the target of the attack. The wielder can then attempt a combat maneuver check to trip his opponent as a free action. This trip attempt does not provoke an attack of opportunity from the creature you are attempting to trip if that creature is not threatening you.

<span id="quick"></span>**Quick:** Can be drawn as a swift action (1 AP) even if hidden; this requires a DC 15 Dexterity check, or DC 10 with Sleight of Hand (if you have at least 3 ranks in Sleight of Hand, you can take 10 on this action in combat).

<span id="hideable"></span>**Hideable:** Easy to hide; nobody knows you are armed unless they search you.

<span id="anti-armor"></span>**Anti-Armor:** Ignores 2 points of AC from armor, but only from objects or natural armor, not from armor obtained through magic.


<span id="lunge-weapon"></span>**Lunge Weapon:** You can use the effects of the Lunge feat with this weapon.

<span id="silent"></span>**Silent:** When you use this ranged weapon to snipe with the Stealth skill, the –20 penalty on your Stealth check to stay hidden is reduced to –10.

<span id="unarmed"></span>**Unarmed:** An “Unarmed” weapon increases you to the next unarmed damage die if you have a class that improves your unarmed damage (such as monk or brawler). This doesn’t take you above the maximum die you can obtain (for a monk, it can’t take you above 2d10 at 20th level).

<span id="useful-reach"></span>**Useful Reach:** A reach weapon that, instead of –4 when used as an improvised weapon to strike at 5 ft., has –2.

<span id="disarm-minus"></span>**Disarm-:** Like disarm, but +1 instead of +2.

<span id="natural"></span>**Natural:** If you have something that makes your natural attack deal more damage than this weapon, you use your natural weapon’s damage but keep the critical and other effects this weapon gives you.

<span id="torturer"></span>**Torturer:** More a torture tool than a weapon, so it gives +4 on rolls that involve torture, and possibly a +1 circumstance bonus to Intimidate if threats of torture are involved, as the GM sees fit.

<span id="monk-double"></span>**Monk Double:** A monk can stack two-weapon fighting and flurry of blows with this weapon, but cannot spend ki to gain extra attacks at the same time.

<span id="anti-parry"></span>**Anti-parry:** Ignores AC added by weapons, and parry and riposte cannot be used against this weapon.

<span id="anti-shield"></span>**Anti-shield:** Ignores 2 points of AC from shields (real, physical ones; the [*shield*](../../magic/spells/shield.md) spell doesn’t count).

<span id="light-flail"></span>**Light Flail:** If you spend a swift action (1 AP), your next attack deals double the weapon’s damage (only the weapon’s: 1d6 becomes 2d6, with no double Strength or anything like that). This attack takes a –1 penalty.

<span id="heavy-flail"></span>**Heavy Flail:** If you spend a move action (1 AP), your next attack deals double the weapon’s damage (only the weapon’s: 1d8 becomes 2d8, with no double Strength or anything like that). This attack takes a –1 penalty.

<span id="versatile"></span>**Versatile:** By spending a swift action (1 AP), it can gain one of these weapon special abilities: Disarm-, Grapple-, Heavy Part (the same swift action (1 AP) activates it; you don’t spend 2 swift actions), or Lunge Weapon.

<span id="heavy-part"></span>**Heavy Part:** By spending a swift action (1 AP), you change how you use your weapon so it deals bludgeoning damage.

<span id="coup"></span>**Coup:** The weapon can increase its critical multiplier by an extra ×1 when making a coup de grace, or you can choose not to provoke an attack of opportunity but have a ×1 lower multiplier.

<span id="group-x"></span>**Group X:** A weapon sold in groups of X, all of which have any modifiers or enchantments placed on them. This is normally for thrown weapons designed to be compact and to come in identical groups. “Reloading” this type of weapon, such as recovering 5 javelins stuck in an enemy, requires a full-round action (3 AP) that provokes an attack of opportunity, next to the corpse with the projectiles (the ones that missed are assumed to be close enough that the full-round action (3 AP) also represents fetching them, unless the GM hates you and wants to mess with you).

<span id="non-finesseable"></span>**Non-Finesseable:** This weapon can’t be used with Weapon Finesse, even if it is a light weapon.

<span id="non-melee"></span>**Non-Melee:** –2 on attacks when using this weapon in melee. If a weapon has non-melee-X, the penalty increases by that X. If it has +X, it basically has less of a penalty.

<span id="angled"></span>**Angled:** This weapon’s attacks are unpredictable or easy to move erratically or at unconventional angles, ignoring 2 points of AC from Dexterity or dodge.

<span id="whip"></span>**Whip:** Counts as a whip for feats and anything else that affects whips.

<span id="automatic-weapon-quality"></span>**Automatic Weapon Quality:** Modern firearms include a new type of firearm—the automatic firearm. A weapon with the automatic weapon quality fires a burst of bullets with a single pull of the trigger, attacking all creatures in a line. This line starts from any corner of your space and extends to the limit of the weapon’s range or until it strikes a barrier it cannot penetrate. When an automatic weapon attacks all creatures in a line, it makes a separate attack roll against each creature in the line. Each creature in the line can only be attacked with one bullet from each burst. Each attack roll takes a –2 penalty to account for recoil, and its attack damage cannot be modified by precision damage or damage-increasing feats such as Vital Strike. Effects that grant concealment, such as fog or smoke, or the blur, invisibility, or mirror image spells, do not foil an automatic weapon’s line attack. If any of the attack rolls threaten a critical hit, confirm the critical for that attack roll alone. An automatic weapon misfires only if all of the attack rolls made misfire. A single attack with an automatic weapon fires 10 bullets. An automatic weapon cannot fire single bullets that target one creature. When taking a full-attack action with an automatic weapon, you can fire as many bursts in a round as you have attacks.

<span id="semi-automatic"></span>**Semi-Automatic:** A semi-automatic weapon normally fires one shot as an attack. However, the user can take a full-attack action to fire twice, as if using the Rapid Shot feat (including taking a -2 penalty on all attacks). If the wielder has the Rapid Shot feat, she can use the additional shot from that as well, but the penalty for all shots fired in that round increases to -6.

<span id="slow-firing"></span>**Slow-Firing:** A slow-firing weapon requires a full-round action (3 AP) to use, and thus cannot be used to make iterative attacks.

<span id="scatter-weapon-quality"></span>**Scatter Weapon Quality:** A weapon with the scatter weapon quality can shoot two different types of ammunition. It can fire normal bullets that target one creature, or it can make a scattering shot, attacking all creatures within a cone. Cannons with the scatter weapon quality only fire grapeshot, unless their descriptions state otherwise. When a scatter weapon attacks all creatures within a cone, it makes a separate attack roll against each creature within the cone. Each attack roll takes a –2 penalty, and its attack damage cannot be modified by precision damage or damage-increasing feats such as Vital Strike. Effects that grant concealment, such as fog or smoke, or the blur, invisibility, or mirror image spells, do not foil a scatter attack. If any of the attack rolls threaten a critical, confirm the critical for that attack roll alone. A firearm that makes a scatter shot misfires only if all of the attack rolls made misfire. If a scatter weapon explodes on a misfire, it deals triple its damage to all creatures within the misfire radius.

<span id="armor-pen-x"></span>**Armor Pen X:** Ignores X points of AC from armor, shields, or natural armor. Instead of the general “all firearms ignore 2”, each firearm now ignores its own number.

<span id="recoil-x"></span>**Recoil X:** Whenever you fire more than 1 shot in the same turn, whether from Rapid Shot or iterative attacks, you take –X on all rolls after the first. Using a one-handed weapon with recoil in two hands reduces the penalty by 1, to a minimum of 0.

<span id="double-barreled"></span>**Double-Barreled:** the two barrels can be fired independently as separate attacks, or both can be fired at once as a standard action (1 AP) (the attack action). A double shot that fires bullets is inaccurate, and takes a –2 penalty on both attacks. A double shot that fires bullets targets only a single creature. It doesn’t multiply Vital Strike, and it doesn’t applies sneak attack or any other type of precision damage more than once.

<span id="long-range"></span>**Long Range:** –3 on attacks at 30 ft. or less.

<span id="attached"></span>**Attached:** The weapon is fixed to your hand or arm. You cannot be disarmed of it, and a strike with it counts as an armed attack (as with the spiked gauntlet).

<span id="finesse"></span>**Finesse:** You can use the Weapon Finesse feat to apply your Dexterity modifier instead of your Strength modifier to attack rolls with this weapon sized for you, even though it isn’t a light weapon.

<span id="light-weapon"></span>**Light Weapon:** This weapon counts as a light weapon for all purposes (two-weapon fighting, Weapon Finesse, and so on), even if its category says otherwise.

<span id="strength"></span>**Strength:** Your Strength modifier applies on damage rolls with this weapon, just as it does for thrown weapons. Bows and slings with a Strength rating can also be used with a lower Strength score, but then take the penalties described in their description.

<span id="improvised"></span>**Improvised:** This is an item that was not made as a weapon. It is treated as an improvised weapon (–4 penalty on attack rolls) unless you are proficient with it; its description lists any special effect.

<span id="tool"></span>**Tool:** *Definition pending:* this weapon doubles as a mundane tool (for harvesting, mining, cutting and similar tasks). The exact house rule has not been written yet.

<span id="hurting"></span>**Hurting:** *Definition pending:* the exact house rule has not been written yet. See the description of each weapon with this property.

<span id="improved-nonlethal"></span>**Improved Nonlethal:** *Definition pending:* an improved version of [Nonlethal](#nonlethal). The exact house rule has not been written yet.

<span id="touch"></span>**Touch:** Attacks with this weapon resolve as touch attacks.
