The shifty noble represents any one of the dozens of aristocrats vying for power and wealth in the city of monuments. Some nobles may hail from families with a long lineage in the city, while others might trace their power to one of the city of monuments’s numerous merchant houses, and still others might have used inherited or hard-earned wealth to buy their way into the Council of Ushers. In the city of monuments, money often speaks louder than words, and those with the coin to spare inevitably rise to power either within the ranks of the council or through the city’s various other facets of political strength. While the council is primarily made up of long-lived families who have ruled the city of monuments for years, new money has managed to grasp some political power in recent times as the city of monuments’s importance in trade continues to increase throughout the region.

When the citizens of the city of monuments established their formal government in 4608 ar and instated the Council of Ushers as a means of checking and balancing the power of the lord-mayor, they scarcely thought their seemingly simple political structure would become the convoluted mess it is today. What were originally 15 seats on the council belonging to the most influential and significant families of the city of monuments have since ballooned into 117 seats filled by blasé nobles, scheming aristocrats, and avaricious merchants. Though there remain some honest members of the council who faithfully seek to represent the will of the city of monuments’s citizenry, such honorable endeavors are more often than not stopped up in the city’s byzantine political machine.

While most of the city’s day-to-day business runs on the up and up, corruption and bribery find their way into numerous facets of the city-state’s governing body. Spies and aristocratic messengers from the walled city often travel to the city of monuments bearing ill will or financial threats against its councilors, swaying corrupt politicians to aid their cause as the rival city-state to the east attempts to absorb one or more of the city of monuments’s holdings. Many of the representatives in the Council of Ushers work toward improving the city of monuments’s holdings and status, though some seek instead to undermine progress and sabotage their counterparts’ efforts, putting personal goals and aspirations before the good of the citizens. Agents from the infernal empire and the walled city spend a significant amount of coin in their attempts to subvert the council and sow seeds of destruction among the city of monuments, and these traitors to the City of Monuments are easily among the most prominent threats to the city.
