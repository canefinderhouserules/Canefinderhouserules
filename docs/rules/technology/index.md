# Technology

Even in a world full of magic, wonders remain that perplex even the most learned of sages. Among the most enigmatic objects are the strange technological items salvaged from the wreckage of an ancient starship: laser pistols, powered armor, cybernetics, nanites, robots and more. These rules let you add science-fiction elements to a fantasy game. Technology does not use magic in any way, so it functions normally in areas of antimagic or primal magic and is unaffected by effects that target magic items; at the same time, it needs power to function, can glitch when old, and can be hindered by dedicated spells.

*Using technology in your game*: you can re-skin any existing magic item to stand in for a technological item (and the reverse: a magic rod that seizes control of a construct could simply use the stats of a robojack). Consider introducing technological items without initially naming them, so the players discover them for themselves, and limit their availability (and the availability of the technology creation feats) so that the sense of wonder is preserved. When designing new technological items, price them as you would a magic item.

## Skills

No new skills are introduced to model how characters interact with technology; rather, existing skills are expanded to allow for such interaction. Without the [Technologist](../feats/technologist.md) feat, a character is treated as untrained in the skill in question when using it on technology.

### Craft (Int)

Those who wish to construct or repair technological items use the Craft (mechanical) skill in conjunction with the technological item crafting feats (see [Crafting High-Tech Items](crafting.md)). Without the Technologist feat, Craft (mechanical) can still be used to craft less advanced forms of technology such as gears, hinges, and pulleys. High-tech crafting facilities are extremely rare, so it is not assumed that PCs have access to them; discuss this with your players before allowing technological crafting into the game.

### Researching Technology

A character can use the following skills to research technological subjects. Other skills may have research applications as well, subject to GM approval.

- **Heal**: Used to identify and understand pharmaceuticals.
- **Knowledge (arcana)**: Although robots are constructs, Knowledge (arcana) cannot be used to identify robots or their abilities and weaknesses.
- **Knowledge (engineering)**: This is the most important skill with regard to technological subjects. Knowledge (engineering) can be used to identify a robot’s abilities and weaknesses. Knowledge (engineering) is also used to identify and understand unknown technological objects in a similar manner to how Spellcraft is used to identify the properties of a magic item. The DC to correctly identify and understand an unknown technological object is equal to the object’s Craft DC. An object with a Craft DC of 15 or less can be automatically identified and understood by someone trained in Knowledge (engineering) who also has the Technologist feat.
- **Knowledge (geography)**: Used for astronomy.

### Disable Device (Dex; no armor check penalty; trained only)

With the Technologist feat, you can use Disable Device to interact safely with explosive devices and disable technological devices and traps.

**Disable Electronic Device**: Disabling an electronically controlled trap or unlocking an electronically locked door is easier if you use an e-pick (see [Technological Gear](../../equipment/technology-gear.md)). Without an e-pick, you take a –5 penalty on any attempt to use Disable Device on an electronic device.

### Linguistics (Int; trained only)

A character with the Technologist feat can attempt a Linguistics check to decipher certain complex messages that appear in the wreckage of the ancient starship. Many of these messages are written in the native language of the ship’s crew, a language spoken only rarely, which cannot be chosen as a bonus language for high Intelligence without GM permission. Some robots may also know it (either in addition to Common or replacing it), depending on the robots’ nature.

## Technological Equipment

Technology from the future (or even the present-day real world) in a fantasy setting should be handled in a manner similar to magic items elsewhere in the game. Many technological items replicate specific spells or magical effects. However, they do not use magic in any way.

### Power Sources

Most technological wonders require energy to function. These items each have a *capacity* score, which indicates the maximum number of charges the item can store at any one time. The number of charges an item consumes when it is used varies from item to item. An item’s capacity can be filled from any power source—like a battery or a generator—as a standard action. When an item is charged, it always takes as many charges from the attached power source as it can hold, filling as close to its capacity as possible. Note that charging an item from a generator is more efficient, as any charges drained from a battery in excess of the number of charges an item can store are lost.

### Color Code

Many technological items follow a color code that organizes similar items (such as nanite hypoguns, force fields, or gravity clips) according to their overall power. There are nine colors in the scale—the effects of an item of any individual color should roughly correspond to the power level of a spell of the associated level. Seven of these colors are associated with the seven skymetals (see [Technological Hazards and Artifacts](hazards.md)). The least of the colors, brown, is associated with base ores, while the greatest of the colors, prismatic, is associated with all of the skymetals. In some technological items, actual skymetals of the appropriate color are used in the creation of the object, but in most, synthetic plastics and metals are used in place of the more valuable skymetals.

| Color | Spell Level Equivalent | Skymetal |
|---|---|---|
| Brown | 1st | none |
| Black | 2nd | adamantine |
| White | 3rd | siccatite |
| Gray | 4th | inubrix |
| Green | 5th | noqual |
| Red | 6th | djezet |
| Blue | 7th | abysium |
| Orange | 8th | horacalcum |
| Prismatic | 9th | all skymetals |

### Timeworn Technology

The equipment described here is presented in full working condition and priced as such. Equipment that has been damaged or degraded over time works less consistently and is worth less money than new technological items. Such equipment is called “timeworn,” and is detailed fully in [Technological Hazards and Artifacts](hazards.md).
