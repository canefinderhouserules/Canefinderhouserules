# Lycanthropy

Few seek the fate of the lycanthrope. Shunned, hated and feared throughout most of the world, these misbegotten monsters of the night live hard and often short lives. Shame plagues the best of them for their nocturnal bouts of amnesia and any forgotten misdeeds they may have committed, while the worst lycanthropes revel in the madness of transformation. This page explores common ways an individual might contract and cure lycanthropy, as well as suggestions for groups seeking to play lycanthropic characters. The [skinwalker](../../characters/races/skinwalker.md) race offers a way to evoke the flavor of a lycanthrope without upsetting campaign balance.

## Contracting Lycanthropy

Lycanthropy is a curse, and while some unscrupulous types may seek this "gift of the moon," voluntary contraction of the disease is rare. Several methods exist by which an individual might contract it.

**Curse**: Lycanthropes sometimes acquire their curse through the fell work of an arcanist or a channeler of the divine. Some demon worshipers boast of a patron's gift, the fullest form of which can turn a devotee fully into a lycanthrope. Other spellcasters claim that the secret to channeling the curse can be found in ancient arcane texts and items, or in long-forgotten magic of the spirit world. Oracles dedicated to the [lunar mystery](../../characters/classes/oracle-mysteries.md#lunar-mystery) tell that the key to unlocking the truest potential of lycanthropy lies in strict adherence to the laws of the lunar cycle and the natural world.

**Infection**: The easiest and most common way to contract lycanthropy is by coming too close to an afflicted creature. Bites, scratches and (in a tiny percentage of cases) ingesting blood from a lycanthropic foe may all result in the malady spreading from one carrier to a new victim. Once exposed, newly made carriers likely don't realize the extent of their affliction until the next full moon, and without divinations their moment of transformation is the first they know of their curse.

**Inheritance**: Creatures born with the blood of lycanthropy fully within them are natural lycanthropes. They are every bit as accursed as afflicted ones, save that they are, for the most part, capable of controlling their bestial urges. They can transform at will and are not so attuned to the phases of the moon, yet the blood of the beast still burns within them, and many turn toward depravity and insatiable hunger. Natural and afflicted lycanthropes tend to bear children born with the curse, and it is a mystery how far down a family line the curse runs before it manifests only as skinwalker children or is diluted to the point of non-expression.

## Curing Lycanthropy

Natural lycanthropes and skinwalkers can't use these methods to rid themselves of their ancestry; for them there is virtually no hope of a cure, save perhaps a *miracle* or *wish*.

**Herbalism**: The most accessible true cure lies in the potent and poisonous leaves of herbs such as wolfsbane and belladonna. Unfortunately for a were-creature, the effects of these remedies are as dangerous and difficult to resist as the curse itself. They are often sold in mysterious solutions, at a significant markup, by snake-oil salespeople.

**Magic**: Spells like *remove disease* and *heal* are more definitive and safer ways to break the curse, as long as they are cast by a holy person of sufficient skill (typically a cleric of 12th level or higher). The window in which such treatments function is short: healers have only 3 days after a victim's exposure to the curse. After the 3-day window has passed, a victim's only chance of a cure through magic is a *remove curse* spell, cast by a cleric of 12th level or higher, during the time of the victim's transformation.

**Other Remedies**: Many folk remedies exist to offer false hope, and in some cases to cause real damage. Many of these "cures" are tantamount to torture, given an air of legitimacy by silver tools or ritual. Beating a subject unconscious on the eve of a full moon may stop her from wreaking havoc when transformed, but it is no cure.

## Lycanthropic Characters

Players hoping to play lycanthropic characters should first discuss this with the GM. The powers of a lycanthrope can be unbalancing for games that assume the standard power level for PCs. In most cases, playing a skinwalker lets a player evoke the flavor of a lycanthrope without throwing off the balance of the campaign. A GM who allows lycanthropic PCs should likely make them natural lycanthropes so the players remain in control of their characters' powers throughout the campaign. Keep the following implications in mind.

**Hunted**: Although lycanthropes are powerful, a band of lycanthropic PCs might find itself the target of dangers normal adventurers would rarely face. Lycanthrope slayers, witch-hunts, bounty hunters and zealous monster killers can all take an interest in were-creatures who make their powers known, so hiding the affliction is usually of utmost importance.

**Silver**: The defenses of were-creatures are formidable, but silver damage reduction is relatively easily overcome, as silver weapons are among the cheapest and most readily available. Poisons and alchemical substances derived from silver also pose a constant threat, as many herbalists and alchemists spend their lives finding new ways to capture, torment or kill were-creatures.

## Hiding Lycanthropy

Most nights of the month an afflicted lycanthrope has little trouble hiding her curse. On nights when the moon is full, it behooves her to have a plan for stifling her bestial urges or at least concealing her identity while her alter ego rampages. The most reliable means of avoiding an accidental rampage is to stay hidden indoors, preferably with the windows boarded and the doors locked using a method beyond the manipulation of animal paws. A trusted friend may provide silver manacles and hold the key till dawn. Getting out of town may be in order, and joining an organization whose meetings follow the lunar cycle can disguise regular absences. Powerful magic items, such as the [amulet of the true form and moon clock](../../magic-items/blood-of-the-moon-items.md), may help prevent unwanted transformation.

## The Moon

The light of the full moon is often said to stir up trouble. Some natural lycanthropes believe the moon draws humanoids back to their primal roots, and that both lycanthropy and lunacy are natural expressions of that primal nature. Others speculate that the moon's cycle is tied to the dark powers of the stars. Either way, the full moon's light is deeply linked to the primal nature of the individual, and some lycanthropes worship the moon while others curse its cycle.

**Unusual Moons**: The rules for lycanthropy here and in the Bestiary assume a world with a single moon whose phases and orbit match those of the real-world moon. In a campaign with several moons, pick one to associate with the primal forces that govern lycanthrope activity, or associate different moons with different forms of lycanthropy (for instance, one moon for werewolves and werebears, another for weretigers and wereboars). If one moon is significantly larger or has a much longer cycle, its effects may be stronger. The simplest solution may be a "werewolf standard time" that governs all lycanthropic activity independent of the moon's position. Discuss with your GM what she intends to do.
