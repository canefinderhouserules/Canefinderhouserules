# Aquatic Rules

!!! note "Source"
    Rules from *Pathfinder Campaign Setting: Aquatic Adventures*. Adjudications for campaigns with significant time spent underwater. Setting-specific lore has been omitted.

## Swimming and Aquatic Adventures

The basics of the Swim skill are unchanged. A creature that uses a move or full-round action to swim makes a Swim check against the DC for the water's conditions. On a success it moves up to one quarter of its land speed with a move action, or half with a double move. On a failure it makes no progress. If a creature already underwater fails by 5 or more (or spends no actions to swim), it sinks or rises according to its buoyancy instead of "going underwater."

- A swimmer can't run, charge or take a 5-foot step.
- Every hour of swimming requires a DC 20 Swim check to avoid fatigue. Swimming is at least as strenuous as marching: extensive swimming requires checks as for a forced march, and continual double moves count as hustling.
- A creature whose buoyancy is *swiftly sinking* can walk on the bottom at half its land speed with no Swim check.

**Swim speed.** A creature with a swim speed moves at full swim speed through normal water without a Swim check, can run, charge and take 5-foot steps, and gains a +8 racial bonus on Swim checks to deal with hazards or special actions. If its swim speed is less than one quarter of its land speed (for example, 10 feet from *monkey fish*), it can swim at one quarter of its land speed instead, but loses the benefits of having a swim speed on those rounds.

### Buoyancy

When a creature fails a Swim check by 5 or more, or spends no actions to swim, it sinks or rises according to its buoyancy.

| State | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Sinking** | Naturally sinks. Typical for land dwellers. |
| **Rising** | Naturally rises to the surface (carrying lots of low-density gear, like wood, or air tanks). |
| **Neutral** | Hangs in place unless it expends effort. |
| **Swiftly sinking** | Gear and body so dense it can walk on the bottom; needs a DC 20 Swim check to swim toward the surface or stay off the bottom (carrying plenty of stone or metal). |
| **Swiftly rising** | Needs a DC 20 Swim check to submerge and stay submerged (a sizable air tank or flotation device, or magic). |

An air tank can be adjusted to hold just enough air for neutral buoyancy, but picking up other objects or breathing from the tank ends that until readjusted. Creatures with the aquatic subtype can maintain neutral buoyancy as a free action on their turn (unless carrying something large with high or low density); creatures with the water subtype have the same buoyancy as water.

**Buoyancy speed.** A creature that badly fails (or doesn't attempt) a Swim check begins to sink or rise at the end of its turn at 10 feet; the speed increases by 10 feet each subsequent round, to a maximum of 30 feet after 3 rounds. This movement provokes attacks of opportunity, and the creature is off-balance while moving this way. Once per round, a creature sinking or rising can stop its momentum with a Swim check as a move action. A creature that continues moving in the direction of its buoyancy under its own power can do so with a successful Swim check without stopping first. If buoyancy changes instantly (jettisoning an item, magic), buoyancy speed adjusts by 10 feet per round in the new direction. A creature that fails the DC 20 Swim check to move against its buoyancy while swiftly sinking or swiftly rising moves 30 feet in the buoyancy direction at once.

**Moving against buoyancy.** Each square of movement in the direction opposite a creature's buoyancy costs twice as much movement. Neutral buoyancy ignores this.

**Drag.** Swimming with an item with significant surface area (a big treasure chest) halves speed (or more, at the GM's discretion). A narrow object like a pole avoids drag if turned to face the direction of travel, but turning it takes a move action.

### Thinking in Three Dimensions

Mark a creature's depth relative to its starting depth on a token, on the map, or use whichever method works best at your table.

- **Flanking.** The usual rules apply, but there are 26 squares adjacent to a given square rather than 8, so many weaker creatures can gang up on one stronger creature.
- **Flexible movement.** Swimmers need not follow a predictable path. Underwater defenders should set proximity traps covering a wide radius rather than depending on one square, and blockades and patrols are harder because defenders guard the surface of a sphere instead of the perimeter of a circle.

## Fighting Underwater

### Physical Attacks

Slashing and bludgeoning melee attacks underwater take a –2 penalty and deal half damage, making piercing weapons best. A creature that is off-balance suffers the same penalty and half damage with piercing weapons too. *Freedom of movement* negates these penalties, as does the *Aquadynamic Focus* feat. Creatures with a natural swim speed from the aquatic or water subtype don't take these penalties with their inherent natural attacks (but extra attacks gained from other sources, like an archetype, still do). Attacks that deal bludgeoning damage by pushing water, such as a kineticist's water blast, take no penalty.

**Ranged attacks.** Ranged attacks take a –2 penalty for every 5 feet of water in addition to normal range penalties. The underwater light and heavy crossbows ignore this penalty but have much shorter range increments. Thrown weapons don't usually work underwater, but a properly prepared dense piercing weapon (stone, metal) can be thrown straight down, counting on its sinking buoyancy. At the end of its maximum range it keeps sinking, but too unpredictably to hit anything.

**Combat maneuvers.** Most work underwater. You take a –2 penalty on grapple checks if you don't have a swim speed (a damage action in a maintained grapple still deals full damage even if your unarmed strike is bludgeoning).

**Cover from the surface.** Creatures attacking through the water's surface treat it as total cover (partially submerged targets get proportionally less). Piercing attacks can penetrate the surface at a –2 penalty. This replaces the Core Rulebook rule on cover from the water's surface and allows spearfishing and harpoons.

### Off-Balance and Prone Underwater

An off-balance creature loses its Dexterity bonus to AC, its opponents gain a +2 bonus on attack rolls against it, and its piercing weapon attacks take a –2 penalty and deal half damage. A creature typically becomes off-balance for a round by failing a Swim check, even with neutral buoyancy.

**Tripping.** A successful trip against a swimming creature forces it to make a Swim check against a DC equal to the trip result; failure makes it off-balance. The target can regain its balance by succeeding at a Swim check to move on its next turn.

### Freedom of Movement

A creature with *freedom of movement* can move and attack normally underwater: it swings weapons without penalties (thrown weapons and ammunition lose the benefit once they leave its possession), lifts objects without drag, walks along the bottom at full speed, and automatically succeeds at all Swim checks since the water offers no significant resistance.

### Spells

- **Casting while holding breath.** A creature holding its breath must succeed at a concentration check (DC 15 + spell level) to cast a spell at all. A spell with a verbal component reduces remaining rounds of breath by 3 (stacking with the reduction for a standard or full-round action); a swift or immediate action spell with a verbal component reduces it by 1. The caster can't finish the spell without enough breath. Mundane components work if stored in watertight containers.
- **Acid.** Conjuration (creation) spells that conjure nonmagical acid (such as *acid arrow*) don't work; evocation spells that deal magical acid damage work normally.
- **Clouds and weather.** Cloud spells (*fog cloud*, *obscuring mist*) don't work underwater; *Murky Spell* creates equivalents. Land weather spells (*ice storm*, *sleet storm*) don't function. Illusions like *twilight haze* work.
- **Cold.** Cold spells that don't conjure ice (*cone of cold*) deal half cold and half piercing damage as ice shards form underwater.
- **Electricity.** Magical electricity works normally, but visible and audible bolts and sparks don't appear. Creatures that fail to identify a ranged touch electricity spell are flat-footed against the first such attack in a fight (not against melee touch spells).
- **Fire.** Normal fire and most supernatural fire can't burn underwater. When casting a fire spell underwater, make a caster level check (DC 20 + spell level) to channel the heat into steam, dealing normal damage without secondary effects like igniting combustibles. A fire spell is either entirely steam or entirely fire; if the area extends above the surface, choose a side, and the surface blocks line of effect for the rest. Spells that already create steam need no check. *Steam Spell* makes this easier.
- **Invisibility.** An invisible creature still displaces water and can be targeted as if it had concealment. *Invisibility bubble* hides the displaced water.
- **Sonic.** Results are the same as on land; at the GM's discretion the caster can double the range or area, halve the damage and lower the DC by 2.

### Perception and Survival

- **Perception.** Perception checks to see are at a minimum –2 underwater, and worse in murky water (flowing water is always at least somewhat murky). Maximum visibility: 4d8 × 10 feet in clear water (40–320 feet), 1d8 × 10 feet in murky water (10–80 feet). Open-water Stealth generally requires being at least that distance away. Surface creatures without goggles take a –4 penalty on Perception checks to see underwater and risk eye damage. Scent works normally for aquatic creatures (land creatures can't follow a trail) and carries farther: keen scent can detect blood up to a mile away. Aquatic tremorsense detects disturbances in the water without touching the same solid surface. Hearing checks get a +2 bonus where ambient noise is unusually low.
- **Survival.** Tracking underwater is hard unless the target moves along the bottom or through something that holds signs (a reef, sargassum). The DC starts at 10 and rises by 1 per 10 minutes after passage (1 per minute in a current). Keen scent creatures can track blood trails from a great distance.

## Underwater Hazards and Features

### Drowning

A creature can typically hold its breath for rounds equal to twice its Constitution score. A standard or full-round action expends 1 additional round of breath, so a creature fighting or double-moving each round lasts rounds equal to its Constitution score. Intentionally exhaling expends 1 round (small exhalation) to 3 rounds (extended, like singing or casting a spell). *Suffocation* expels all held breath.

When out of breath, the creature must make a Constitution check each round (DC 10, +1 per round after the first). On a failure it begins to drown: unconscious (and 0 hit points if it had more), –1 hit points the next round, dead the round after. If it can breathe again, drowning stops but no hit points are regained. A creature holding its breath where it could breathe normally doesn't fall unconscious.

Ways around it: *water breathing* (long-lasting, protects a group), *air bubble* (a few minutes for one creature), *life bubble* (half the duration of *water breathing* but protects against temperature and pressure), or air tanks (carrying many extra breaths sets buoyancy to swiftly rising).

### Oceanic Zones

| Zone | Depth | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Sunlight** | Surface to 600 ft. | Sufficient light for plants; the visibility estimates above assume this. Temperature varies widely (over 100° F to below 30° F). |
| **Twilight** | 600–3,300 ft. | About 1% of surface light; low-light creatures see 60 ft. as dim light. About 68° F at the top to 39° F at the bottom. |
| **Midnight** | 3,300–13,000 ft. | No light; needs darkvision or nonvisual senses. About 39° F. Often reaches the bottom. |
| **Abyssal** | 13,000 ft. to the bottom | Dark and cold (35° to 39° F); hydrothermal vents provide warmth. |
| **Abaddonian** (optional) | Below 20,000 ft. | Deep trenches; pressures over 1,000 times surface, cold and ambient magic. |

**Pressure.** Most aquatic creatures ignore pressure damage within their native depth range unless they travel instantly between depths (as with *dimension door*). Pressure damage is as in the Core Rulebook. Surface creatures treat 0–100 feet as their native zone. For every minute spent below their native zone, a creature must make a Fortitude save (DC 15, +1 per minute after the first) or take 1d6 damage plus 1d6 for every additional 100 feet beyond the first. After five successful saves (consecutive or not) the creature adapts, shifting its native range down or up by 100 feet; a creature within its native range suffering an instantaneous change adapts all at once. A surface dweller who descends and adapts will suffer pressure hazards again when ascending.

*Example:* swimming to 220 feet is 120 feet outside the native range of 0–100, so 2d6 damage each minute on a failed save. After five saves the range becomes 100–200 (1d6 per minute), and after five more, 200–300 (no damage), but ascending to the surface restores 2d6 damage until adapted again.

*Freedom of movement* and *life bubble* prevent pressure damage; *pressure adaptation* and *stabilize pressure* are lower-level options.

**Temperature.** Creatures in water at 80° F or higher are safe from hypothermia.

| Water temperature | Equivalent |
|---|---|
| 60° to 80° F | Cold weather: Fortitude save each hour or 1d6 nonlethal cold damage |
| 40° to 60° F | Severe cold: Fortitude save every 10 minutes or 1d6 nonlethal cold damage |
| 32° to 40° F | Extreme cold: automatic 1d6 lethal cold damage per minute |
| Below 32° F (high salt) | Automatic 2d6 lethal cold damage per minute |

Sea creatures are adapted to their native zones. *Endure elements* protects against water as cold as 32° F. Water rarely gets hot enough to harm except near hazards like hydrothermal vents.

### Currents and Running Water

Currents work like rivers: each has a speed (feet per round), width and direction, and pushes a creature that distance at the start of its turn. This movement provokes attacks of opportunity from creatures not in the same current. Swimming perpendicular to the flow is usually best. Natural ocean currents rarely exceed 50 feet per round in the sunlight zone and are slower with depth (rivers can reach 90). A current is at least rough water (Swim DC 15); faster currents are at least stormy (DC 20). Most of the ocean is not running water, but currents are (so *locate creature* is blocked by even a slow current, and vampires risk being affected by an unexpected current).

### Whirlpools

Currents flowing in opposite directions meet to form whirlpools. Most are harmless, but powerful maelstroms reach speeds around 200 feet per round, pulling in nearby creatures and preventing escape. Whirlpool movement doesn't normally provoke attacks of opportunity. Swim checks reach DC 40 or higher. Creatures take 1d6 bludgeoning damage per round for every 50 feet of whirlpool speed. A natural whirlpool eventually drags captives to the bottom; sometimes the only way to survive is to reach the center.
