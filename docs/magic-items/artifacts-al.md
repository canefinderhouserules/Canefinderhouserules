# Artifacts and Legends

A catalog of legendary artifacts, with guidance on how characters can learn about them and a few utility artifacts a GM can use to solve out-of-game problems. Names of deities, places and peoples have been made neutral.

## Artifacts in Your Game

Artifacts deliberately leave gaps in the rules so a GM can tell the stories he wants. Whenever an artifact appears, the GM should know how the players can learn more about it.

### Learning About Artifacts

**Spellcraft.** Artifacts are magic items and can be identified with Spellcraft, but the DC is based on caster level (typically DC 35 or more). The GM can raise the DC by +10 or more, or declare an artifact unidentifiable this way, especially for artifacts alien to the world or lost for millennia, which characters may never have encountered documents about (they can then identify only the schools and general power level).

**Knowledge.** The DC of a Knowledge check to learn about an artifact is 15 + the artifact's caster level + ad hoc adjustments. The GM chooses which Knowledge skill (or skills) applies; Knowledge (arcana) should not be a catch-all. Bards using bardic knowledge are particularly adept. As with identifying monsters, a GM can reveal only the basics on a success and an additional, more obscure detail for every 5 points by which the result exceeds the DC.

**Magical identification.** *Identify* and *analyze dweomer* do not reveal an artifact's abilities. *Legend lore* is one of the few useful spells, but it describes history and tales rather than concrete game terms, so an artifact retains its mystery.

**Research.** Because artifacts are unique, a character's chance of having already read about one is effectively nil. Deliberate research in a specialized place of learning (a sage's collection, a city library, a university or a hidden trove) takes 1 week, after which the character makes a Knowledge check as above. The GM may grant a +2 bonus for a well-stocked library up to +5 for a collection with unique resources and a helpful staff (more if peerless). A success reveals the artifact's history and abilities (or a portion); a failure reveals nothing and the GM may decide that collection simply does not hold the information. Not every collection is significant enough; a noble's collection of romances is not appropriate.

**Sages.** Some artifacts are unknown even to legend. A sage is anyone with special knowledge of the artifact: a scholar with a very specific field, a tome, a mummy that lived through a relevant time, or the artifact's creator. A sage is the only way to learn, and finding the sage can be an adventure in itself.

**Destruction.** An artifact's destruction method is not usually known by its bearer. The GM may allow research: at least 1 week in a world-renowned lore collection and a successful Knowledge check (DC 20 + the artifact's caster level).

### Utility Artifacts

These four artifacts address real-world concerns of running a game. They have no history or set gold value, should not be sought by NPCs as plot MacGuffins, and are boons a GM might grant early.

#### Figurine of the Concealed Companion

**Aura** strong transmutation; **CL** 15th; **Slot** none; **Weight** 1 lb.

In its natural form this looks like a fist-sized hunk of distinctly colored clay. When taken up by a character with a deep bond to a creature bound by the animal companion, familiar or mount class feature, and brought into contact with that creature, the creature vanishes and the clay becomes a miniature replica of it sculpted from colored stone. Once bound, the character can toss down the figurine and speak a command word to return the creature to its natural state and size. While the creature is in its natural form its owner can speak the word to turn it back into a miniature. In figurine form a creature does not age, perceive time or need to breathe, eat or drink, and can be kept so indefinitely. If the bound creature is killed, the figurine returns to unshaped clay and can bond to another creature.

*Use* Keeps companions safe when not needed and lets mounts through tight areas.

#### Scar of Destiny

**Aura** strong conjuration; **CL** 20th; **Slot** none; **Weight** —

This scar appears on specific individuals at birth or through powerful, unexplained magical confluences. Someone with the mark is untethered from reality and might step out of existence at any moment for indeterminate periods, possibly forever. All of the individual's possessions vanish with him, though occasionally specific items of the bearer's choosing are left behind. When he reappears he is unharmed, knows nothing of where he was, and has only a vague idea of how much time has passed; such individuals usually reappear near others who bear the mark.

*Use* The scar affects all PCs in a party from character creation. When a player can't make a session, it gives the GM a way to remove the character from the game without playing the absent PC or having another player run him.

#### Sliver of the Amalgam Mind

**Aura** strong divination; **CL** 20th; **Slot** none; **Weight** —

A tiny clear crystal, hardly longer than a fingernail, part of a great otherworldly device that shattered long ago. Alone it has no power. Brought into contact with another sliver, both turn the same color. If the slivers are then inserted into the skin of sentient individuals, those bearing shards of the same color can communicate telepathically with any other creature pierced by a matching shard within line of sight. They hear only what others wish them to, and broadcast only what they wish; a bearer has complete control and can block the telepathy or broadcast only to specific individuals.

*Use* Players converse around the table and sometimes act on information their characters can't know; this gives the PCs a reason to know it.

#### Hourglass of Transfiguration

**Aura** strong (all schools); **CL** 20th; **Slot** none; **Weight** —

Whoever breaks this crystalline hourglass blacks out for 24 hours. During that time no power can wake her, as she is gradually swapping places with another version of herself from some other place (a parallel dimension, an ideal concept of the gods, an impossibly distant time). If a PC breaks it, her player may completely recreate the character, rerolling statistics, changing race, class, feats and skills, and altering any other choice, as major or minor as she pleases. Her possessions stay the same. After 24 hours the character reawakens, revised and healed as by *heal*. Her memories are nearly identical to those of her previous self, though age and certain choices might cause numerous minor differences. The effect is permanent; the previous incarnation can be restored only by finding another hourglass of transfiguration.

*Use* Gives players seeking new options renewed investment in a game.

## Major Artifacts

### Plague Prophet's Ring

**Aura** strong divination and necromancy (evil); **CL** 20th; **Slot** ring; **Weight** —

On donning the ring the wearer becomes the host of an infectious disease (the Mark of the Prophet) but is immune to its effects. Any creature that physically contacts the wearer (or one afflicted by the disease) must succeed at a Fortitude save or be infected. The disease deals 1d4 points of ability damage to the wearer's highest ability score (or her choice in case of a tie) on the victim. The save DC is 10 + the wearer's Hit Dice + her Charisma modifier. It can be cured as normal; creatures immune to disease are immune to it but gain none of the ring's abilities.

Every infected creature becomes a potential host for the wearer's senses. The wearer always knows how many creatures are infected and can detect any one of them as a standard action as per *status*. By concentrating 1 minute she can see and hear through any infected individual's senses (as *clairaudience/clairvoyance*), remaining dimly aware of her surroundings, and can end it as an immediate action. She may do this any number of times per day. Once per day she can either cure any number of infected individuals within 300 feet or force them to make an additional save against the disease at a –5 penalty. Removing the ring or killing the wearer instantly cures all creatures afflicted by the disease.

**Destruction** The ring shatters if the dread patron of pestilence it is bound to is slain. Alternatively, it is destroyed if a creature immune to disease wears it and casts *remove disease* on at least one victim of every different disease known to the world.

### Blightaxe

**Aura** strong conjuration; **CL** 20th; **Slot** none; **Weight** 50 lbs.

Noxious acid seeps from the obsidian blade of this gigantic black greataxe. It is a Huge +5 *plant bane* greataxe, considered epic, which can penetrate epic damage reduction. Virulent acid constantly seeps from the blade, dealing +1d6 points of acid damage on a hit and an additional +2d10 acid damage on a critical hit. Once per minute the wielder can use *heal* on herself by striking a living, nonmagical tree with the axe, which withers to ash.

The artifact is inexorably tied to the nascent demon lord who forged it, who can call it to his hand as a free action over any distance unless it is kept in an area that prevents teleportation. While another creature wields it, the demon lord always knows that creature's location and condition (as *discern location* and *status*) and by concentrating can observe the wielder's surroundings through the wielder's senses. In this state he may use any of his spell-like abilities through the link, treating the axe as the source. Each time he does so the wielder must succeed at a DC 25 Will save or be staggered 1 round from the flow of power. These spell-like abilities trigger during the wielder's turn in initiative but consume none of her actions.

**Destruction** The axe must be used against a powerful magical tree, and while the sap is still fresh on the blade it must then be used to sever one of the many fungoid limbs of the demon lord whose realm it was carved from. It then explodes in a burst of obsidian shrapnel and acid dealing 20d6 acid and 20d6 piercing damage to all within 60 feet (DC 30 Reflex half).

### Heroic Organ Jars

**Aura** strong abjuration and necromancy (lawful); **CL** 20th; **Slot** none; **Weight** 2 lbs. each

Four grotesquely shaped red clay canopic jars that bleed as though living organs, each containing the still-living organ of a fallen herald of a dead god. Each retains a lingering power of that god. A cleric who bears one is granted an additional domain, with its granted powers and domain spells, for as long as he possesses it; a non-cleric gets only the lowest-level domain power. The bearer is also inspired by some trait of the herald and gains an ability she possessed in life, without needing to meet prerequisites.

| Organ | Domain | Trait |
|---|---|---|
| Heart | Protection | Love: gain *lay on hands* as a paladin of your character level |
| Liver | Glory | Faith: gain Greater Sunder as a bonus feat |
| Lung | Knowledge | Duty: gain Improved Iron Will as a bonus feat |
| Spleen | Law | Valor: gain Weapon Specialization (longsword) as a bonus feat |

**Destruction** Together, a powerful undead tyrant and the herald (now a lich) can destroy any of the jars, binding the organ inside into the herald's withered form in a 24-hour ritual.

### Tome of the Damned

**Aura** overwhelming (all schools; chaotic, lawful, evil); **CL** 25th; **Slot** none; **Weight** 40 lbs.

This massive collection of folios, leathery scrolls and manuscripts, bound in bladed steel, stretched flesh and compressed ashes, is the multiverse's greatest collection of profane lore, detailing the evil planes and their denizens beyond what a tome of its size could physically contain. It consists of scattered chapters divided into three volumes (daemonic, demonic and diabolic). Only when all chapters of all three volumes are united does it exhibit the following abilities.

The complete tome radiates a permanent *unhallow* effect in a 150-foot radius and a *sympathy* effect tuned to attract all evil creatures. Any good creature that willingly touches it must succeed at a DC 20 Fortitude save or be slain. While the tome is outside its repository, any evil being of demigod-level power or greater using *scrying* or a similar spell can view it without risk of detection by the bearer. Any creature that makes use of any of its abilities is **damned**, condemned to one of the Lower Planes after death regardless of deeds; only the intervention of a deity can prevent this.

*Talisman.* While carried, the owner casts all spells with the evil descriptor as if two caster levels higher and gains a +5 bonus on all Charisma-based skill checks when interacting with evil outsiders. The bearer can use these spell-like abilities: at will, *align weapon* (evil only), *bestow curse*, *detect evil*, *speak with dead*; 3/day, *banishment*, *blasphemy*, *dictum*, *magic circle*, *unhallow*, *unholy aura*, *unholy blight*, *word of chaos*; 1/day, *dimensional lock*, *gate*, *greater planar binding*, *soul bind*.

*Reference.* A character who reads Abyssal, Celestial and Infernal and spends a total of 30 days (not necessarily contiguous) studying it learns soul-tainting secrets. The bearer gains a +10 bonus on Knowledge (planes) checks whenever she consults the book for 1 hour about a question. Its descriptions of the evil planes are so vivid that any teleportation to or within them always brings the caster to the desired location (no familiarity roll). The bearer gains a +5 bonus on the opposed Charisma check required by *planar binding* (stacking with the +5 on interactions with evil outsiders). Once per day the bearer can ask the tome a question about a profane topic and opens it to a random page to find the answer, revealed as by *vision*. The book also contains a copy of every spell with the evil descriptor.

*Repository.* Once per day the bearer can make the tome vanish into its own internal demiplane, leaving a sinister shimmering portal. This reproduces *mage's magnificent mansion*, admitting the bearer and those she chooses to an unnerving but semi-safe library of countless blasphemous records. The tome and everyone within are affected by a powerful *nondetection*, shielded even from the gods. Creatures do not age inside, but age an amount equal to the time spent on leaving. Visitors may access any power in the Reference section. Non-evil creatures must succeed at a DC 30 Will save every time they try to rest within or be afflicted as by *nightmare* (nothing can prevent this). During lunar eclipses and the midnight hour of a new moon night, the portal appears as a normal door that creatures other than those the bearer chooses can enter.

**Destruction** The tome must be divided into its chapters and each destroyed separately within 1 day. The daemonic chapter must be scrubbed of ink by the tears of a good outsider; the pages of the demonic chapter must be fed to a good or lawful outsider; and the diabolic chapter must serve as kindling for a pyre that burns a good or chaotic outsider alive. If all chapters are not destroyed by the end of the day, any destroyed chapters reappear undamaged in an obscure corner of the multiverse.

### Thornheart Blade

**Aura** strong conjuration, necromancy and transmutation; **CL** 20th; **Slot** none; **Weight** 6 lbs.
**Alignment** CN; **Ego** 23; **Senses** 120 ft., darkvision; **Int** 10, **Wis** 10, **Cha** 16; **Communication** empathy, speech, telepathy; **Languages** Aklo, Common, Sylvan

This bastard sword appears grown rather than forged, its elaborate hilt and blade set with strange gem-like growths in deeper-than-emerald shades and blooming with fine silver. It is an intelligent +5 cold iron *vorpal* bastard sword whose special purpose is to defeat denizens of the fey realm. It is the living embodiment of the passions of the fey queen it was torn from.

**Powers** 3/day, *barkskin* (wielder only), *blight* (swift action, on a creature it strikes in combat); 1/month, *true resurrection* (wielder only).

**Destruction** If the blade is claimed by the fey queen it was torn from, it gradually rejoins her; if left in her possession it fades out of existence over a week, destroying the weapon and restoring her ability to love. Only one of the ancient fey lords can restore the blade once she has reclaimed it.

### Walking Hut

**Aura** overwhelming conjuration and transmutation; **CL** 30th; **Slot** none; **Weight** 3,500 lbs.

A crude hut about 15 feet tall and 15 feet square, hung with drying herbs and etched with eerie symbols, rising on a pair of gigantic chicken legs that endlessly scratch at its surroundings when not tucked beneath it. Its only door opens into a room more than twice the size the structure could naturally contain, a threshold to countless extradimensional rooms. Nothing that happens outside affects those inside.

*Defensive.* At its most basic the hut is a unique CR 17 Huge construct. A creature inside can command it to move, attack, use its special abilities or perform any function it is capable of through its controls: a brown hen's egg in a cracked clay bowl on a table inside. Both can be removed or destroyed but reappear 1 hour later and function only inside the hut. A creature that succeeds at a DC 30 Use Magic Device check can use the controls; a failure makes the hut dance erratically (possibly trampling nearby creatures), and a new check is needed every round to control it directly. It can be given simple standing commands (attack any humanoid that approaches, patrol, head in a direction, flee if damaged) but not multipart instructions or recognition of specific individuals.

*Transportive.* The hut can slip between planes and even planets, carrying everything inside. A creature stands before the hut's cauldron, places two ingredients inside and stirs the stew (a full-round action). The two ingredients are "keys" to a specific destination (a combination, or "recipe"); the keys appear to be mundane items stored throughout the hut, but only those specific items inside it function. Keys can be removed or destroyed but reappear 1 hour later. Once the keys are placed, the hut immediately appears at the destination as if by *plane shift* or interplanetary teleport, always in the same location for that destination, and the keys vanish and reform elsewhere in the hut. Each destination has its own corresponding interior layout (every layout contains one room with the cauldron and controls); other layouts remain inaccessible from within but can be visited with *plane shift*. Its builder and perhaps other residents can travel freely among all its rooms regardless of the hut's location. GMs can create a list of destinations and key pairs.

**Destruction** If the cauldron is used to open a gate within the cauldron itself, a rift in reality opens, destroying the extradimensional interior and sucking in the exterior frame, likely destroying whoever activated the cauldron. Only its maker knows the recipe, one ingredient of which is her own left eye.

### Helm of the Black Dragon

**Aura** overwhelming (varied); **CL** 22nd; **Slot** head; **Weight** 8 lbs.

An intimidating helm of scarred, sigil-etched copper bearing a pair of sweeping dragon horns, crafted from splinters of the bones of a ravener. Whenever the wearer touches a living creature (even as part of casting a spell or a touch attack), the touch deals an additional 5d6 points of negative energy damage and the wearer gains that many hit points as if she had cast *vampiric touch*. The wearer is immune to acid and can see perfectly in darkness of any kind, even magical. When worn by an undead creature, the helm halves all damage taken from positive energy.

Once per day the wearer can order the helm to transform into an ancient black dragon that obeys unquestioningly for 1 hour before reverting. Slaying the dragon does not destroy the helm but prevents the ability from being used for 1 week. While in dragon form, the owner receives none of the helm's other benefits.

**Destruction** The helm can be destroyed by crushing it between the jaws of an ancient black dragon's skull in the middle of a desert with the sun at its zenith.

### Envious Pendant

**Aura** strong abjuration and divination; **CL** 20th; **Slot** neck; **Weight** 1 lb.

A dazzling diamond glitters within this amulet's dark metal setting, whose designs resemble fangs and chains. The pendant rewards devotion and punishes betrayal. When first given to a creature, it recognizes members of that creature's race and gender (not a specific individual) as its owner. The owner automatically fails any saving throw against divination magic, but the pendant protects her, reactively casting spells to defend against effects that compromise her control, each 3 times per day: *freedom* (in response to imprisonment, petrification, paralysis and similar effects), *greater dispel magic* (in response to fear or mind-affecting effects), *remove curse* (in response to curses) and *protection from chaos* (in response to any possession or control). The wearer has no control over when these are cast, and the target is always the wearer.

If worn by a creature not of the recognized race and gender, it ceases to defend. Additionally, once every 1d6 days it makes a self-destructive *suggestion*: the wearer must succeed at a DC 20 Will save or make a casual attempt to harm herself during the day (not total control; she might cut herself if holding a weapon or jump if she passes a high window). If she resists three times in a row, the pendant conjures a miniature *prismatic sphere* into her throat, causing suffocation; it can be removed only by the spells that remove a full-size *prismatic wall*. The recognized owner can be reset by casting *mage's disjunction* on the artifact, with the normal risk of destroying it; whoever receives it next becomes the new owner. The pendant is under a permanent *nondetection* (affecting only the artifact, not the wearer).

**Destruction** It must be offered to 100 free-willed mortals who know nothing of its powers and be rejected by each.

### Binding Lens

**Aura** overwhelming abjuration; **CL** 22nd; **Slot** none; **Weight** 10 lbs.

A disk of flawless glass in a silver band engraved with countless miniscule runes. At its most basic, it is the focal point for an inward-facing *magic circle* of the user's choice (of any radius) that can hold one outsider called by *planar binding* regardless of size. It does not cast the spell; another agency must cast *planar binding*, using the lens as an additional focus component. The target gets its normal Will save, but once it appears within the circle it cannot escape: the lens prevents it from using spell resistance or Charisma to break free and locks it in place as *dimensional anchor*. It remains trapped indefinitely until it agrees to the user's demands or a sentient creature touches the prison with intent to free it (even a roll of 1 on a Charisma check to coerce it does not free it). The lens grants no bonus on Charisma checks to influence the trapped creature.

*Engine.* The outsider can instead be bound within the lens, turning the artifact into an arcane engine. The user must compel the outsider to submit as with *planar binding*; the outsider receives a +10 bonus on the opposed Charisma check to resist, reduced by 1 for every week it remains bound (minimum 0). The lens holds only one outsider at a time and cannot use its magic circle effect while an outsider is trapped. A bound outsider is trapped inside the lens and cannot free itself. While bearing the lens, the wielder can use the outsider's spell-like abilities. The outsider's Hit Dice become a pool of **soul points** (1 per HD); each use costs soul points equal to the ability's spell level, and if the lens lacks enough points the ability cannot be used. Per-time frequency limits of the outsider still apply, and the pool replenishes every day. The outsider can be freed by spending 1 soul point; the released outsider is exhausted but not controlled by the bearer. The outsider can also communicate telepathically with a creature touching the artifact, though it exerts no control.

**Destruction** The lens can be destroyed only if a deity is freed from within it. It is unclear whether a deity could be bound in by normal means, but such a being's release would be so explosive as to shatter the lens.

### Lesser Orbs of Dragonkind

The ten true Orbs of Dragonkind appear in the core artifact list. Lesser variants with limited powers also exist.

#### Orb of Dragon Mastery

**Aura** strong enchantment; **CL** 20th; **Slot** none; **Weight** 4 lbs.

These grim orbs swirl hypnotically with crimson and the color of the draconic breed whose minds they ensnare. The bearer can, as a standard action, *dominate monster* on dragons of the orb's variety within 250 feet (DC 23 Will save; spell resistance is no use). By steeping the orb for 1 hour in the blood of a true dragon dead no more than a day, the DC increases by the slain dragon's age category (an old dragon gives +8). The bonus decreases by 1 each day until the orb returns to its normal DC or is steeped again. It cannot be steeped in the blood of multiple dragons for an increased benefit, but the blood can come from any breed of true dragon.

**Destruction** A dragon of the color the orb controls must willingly swallow it, which kills the dragon and shatters the artifact.

#### Orb of Dragonkin

**Aura** strong enchantment; **CL** 19th; **Slot** none; **Weight** 4 lbs.

Each of these orbs holds power over a specific race of lesser draconic creatures with the dragon type (except true dragons). The bearer can, as a standard action, *dominate monster* on a creature of the orb's variety within 200 feet (DC 23 Will save; spell resistance is no use). The orb grants the AC and saving throw bonuses of the associated draconic creature, replacing the character's own values whether better or worse; they cannot be modified except by being rid of the orb. The owner always knows if draconic creatures of the type are within 10 miles. Each orb also has a power invokable once per round at caster level 10th:

### Relics of the Dragon Tyrant

Seven blasphemous intelligent artifacts, each forged from the remains of a dreaded evil blue dragon tyrant. All are lawful evil unless noted, with Ego 10, senses 30 ft., empathy communication, and (unless stated) Int 10, Wis 10, Cha 12. Each is a potent artifact on its own. If one is destroyed, the original bone remains, losing all powers but retaining the dragon's lingering consciousness; to truly destroy all seven, the dragon must be brought back to life and slain.

**Armor of Skulls** — *Aura* strong abjuration and transmutation; *CL* 20th; *Slot* armor; *Weight* 30 lbs.; Int 10, Wis 12, Cha 10. A Large intelligent +4 *greater electricity resistance* breastplate granting a +4 enhancement bonus to Strength and Constitution. Once per day the wearer can unleash a 120-foot line of electricity (24d8, Reflex DC 37 half) or a 30-foot-radius burst (6d8, Reflex DC 37 half). Powers 3/day: *fear* (DC 16), *freedom of movement*, *protection from energy*, *shout* (DC 16). **Destruction** One who has never killed another creature must bind the armor in gold chains worth 1,000,000 gp, then slowly shatter it by shortening the chain by one link every day for 1,000 days.

**Bound Blade** — *Aura* strong necromancy; *CL* 20th; *Weight* 6 lbs.; Int 10, Wis 10, Cha 12; constant *death watch*, 3/day *deeper darkness*, *rage*, *slow* (DC 14). A +3 *unholy* adamantine bastard sword that grants a +6 enhancement bonus to Strength. As a free action when it reduces a sentient living creature to 0 or fewer hit points, the wielder can cast *soul bind*, forcing a DC 25 Will save or the soul is trapped in the sword. While a soul is trapped, the sword emanates *desecrate* in a 50-foot-radius aura for a number of days equal to the slain creature's Hit Dice; afterwards the soul is destroyed and cannot be restored short of divine intervention. The wielder can release a trapped soul by using the blade's *soul bind* on another creature. **Destruction** It shatters if used by a paladin of the god of law and civilization to slay a fallen paladin dedicated to a god of pain.

**Crown of Fangs** — *Aura* strong enchantment, illusion, necromancy; *Slot* head; *Weight* 4 lbs.; Int 10, Wis 10, Cha 12; 3/day *alter self*, *dominate person* (DC 17), *major image* (DC 17), *mirror image*, *mislead* (DC 19). Teeth set in a jeweled gold circlet, granting a +6 enhancement bonus to Constitution and Charisma, regeneration 20 (only a holy avenger, a specific legendary holy sword, or a paladin's smite evil can deal lethal damage to the wearer), and the personal benefits of *foresight*. **Destruction** It is destroyed if struck by a holy sword forged by a once-mortal god.

**Howling Horn** — *Aura* strong evocation; *CL* 20th; *Weight* 12 lbs.; Int 10, Wis 10, Cha 12; 3/day *circle of death* (DC 19), *repulsion* (DC 19), *waves of exhaustion*. A deadly musical instrument shaped from the dragon's horns, granting a +4 enhancement bonus to Dexterity and Charisma. Sounding it forces all nonevil creatures within 40 feet to make a DC 25 Fortitude save or be deafened 2d6 rounds. ### Demiplane Scroll

**Aura** overwhelming conjuration; **CL** 23rd; **Slot** none; **Weight** 3 lbs.

This innocuous scrap of parchment bears a single large elegant rune that is the physical manifestation of a man-made demiplane; the rune represents its several island regions. Those who fail are sucked into the scroll and bodily transported to one of the waypoints within the demiplane. **Destruction** Containing even a small group of chaotic beings (azatas, proteans, demons) gradually causes the demiplane to unravel; a larger group or a more powerful chaotic entity can rend it to shreds in moments.

### Shattered Shield of the Faithful

### Crown of the Holy Warrior

**Aura** overwhelming conjuration and evocation; **CL** 21st; **Slot** head; **Weight** 1 lb.

Shards of broken metal pierce this splintered wooden circlet, part of a helmet that violently shattered. It functions as a major *crown of blasting* that grants the wearer a deflection bonus to AC equal to her Charisma modifier. In the hands of a paladin it reveals its true powers, bursting into flames and spinning above the head as a halo of divine power. Paladins of any faith who wear it have their class abilities enhanced: they gain three additional mercies (from levels they have access to but have not selected), healing the maximum possible every time they use *lay on hands*, and a number of additional uses of *smite evil* equal to their Charisma modifier. If a paladin wearing the crown is slain, she may choose to be instantly restored to full hit points for a number of minutes equal to her Charisma modifier; she acts normally but at the end of the time dies and cannot be restored short of a *miracle* cast by a cleric of her faith.

Even those who succeed are treated as evil creatures and gain negative levels. Paladins who receive *atonement* can wear it again.

**Destruction** If a paladin temporarily restored to life by the crown forsakes her deity and freely swears herself to an evil force, the crown consumes the wearer in holy flames, destroying itself and its wearer utterly; nothing can restore a traitorous paladin killed this way.

### Shapeshifting Blade

**Aura** strong conjuration; **CL** 20th; **Slot** none; **Weight** 2 lbs.

An elegant blue-tinged blade with three cabochons in the crossguard and a hilt that appears woven of hair-thin metallic fibers. Despite its best-known name, it is not necessarily a rapier: it is a +3 *keen* *speed* sword whose type varies from wielder to wielder. When taken up by a new owner it transforms into any type of sword of the wielder's choosing, mundane or exotic, and from then on transforms into that type whenever held by that individual. The wielder gains a +4 bonus on Bluff and Diplomacy checks to influence humanoids, and can use the blade like a compass to point toward the nearest collection of gems, silver or drinkable water as a standard action at will.

The sword lets the wielder slip or tighten the bonds of the physical world, casting *dimension door*, *dimensional lock* or *greater teleport*. These can drain the sword for the day: *dimensional lock* and *greater teleport* each can be used once per day and drain it immediately; *dimension door* can be used at will but drains it after 3 uses, although the wielder can continue to use *dimension door* even when drained (the only effect that works while drained). A drained sword loses all magical properties (apart from *dimension door*), including its +3 bonus, skill bonuses and *keen* and *speed*. All properties are restored every day at midnight. Gnomes gain a +2 bonus on Knowledge or Spellcraft checks to identify it; fey recognize it as of fey-realm craftsmanship.

**Destruction** It is destroyed if taken into a demiplane where time doesn't pass; earlier incarnations of the weapon continue to exist.

## Lesser Artifacts

Minor artifacts are typically artifact-level treasures of which multiple copies exist. They are no less dangerous than major artifacts and the GM should consider the same issues in adding them. They supplement the minor artifacts in the core artifact list.

### Bone House

**Aura** strong conjuration and necromancy; **CL** 17th; **Slot** none; **Weight** 2 lbs.

A variant of *instant fortress* favored by elite agents of dark faiths. It looks like a skull-topped fortress statuette the size of a clenched fist. When a command word is spoken it grows in 1 round into a tower 20 feet square and 30 feet high with arrow slits and a crenellated battlement. The bone walls extend 10 feet into the ground, rooting it, and have hardness 10 and 100 hit points (repaired by *inflict* spells: 10 points of negative energy repairs 1 point of damage). The small door opens only at the owner's command (not even *knock* works) and opens and closes instantly at his command. Creatures nearby (except the owner) caught in the growth take 10d6 damage (DC 19 Reflex half). The interior is under a constant *desecrate* effect and counts as an altar to a dark god for the purposes of desecrate modifiers.

Once expanded, the owner can create up to four mummies per month from drifts of bone ash and detritus in the inner rooms. Creating one takes 10 minutes and the owner must be inside the entire time; because of *desecrate* the mummies gain +2 hit points per Hit Die. They guard the contents without commands (other tasks require other means to command them); no more than four can be active at once. It is deactivated by speaking a different command word, and only when empty.

**Destruction** It can be destroyed largely by mundane means, but its final hit point must be taken by a positive energy effect (a *cure* spell or similar cast on the nearly ruined structure).

### Crown of the Simurgh

**Aura** strong divination and transmutation (good); **CL** 18th; **Slot** head; **Weight** 1 lb.

A crown of the simurgh grants the wearer the protection of the simurgh that created it. It works only for the single creature for whom it was specifically crafted, and a simurgh can create one every 500 years. The wearer is constantly under *protection from evil*. Once per day she can spend a full-round action concentrating to mentally contact the simurgh; if the simurgh allows the connection, the wearer instantly knows its distance and direction and can communicate as per *sending*. If the simurgh is on the same plane and both wish it, the wearer can teleport to the simurgh's location; 10 minutes later she teleports back to where she was before (or the nearest safe square), whether she wishes to or not. As a final effort in a time of great need, she can take a full-round action to hold the crown to the sun and call out to the simurgh, summoning it as per *summon monster IX* for 1 minute (it follows her commands); at the end of this time the simurgh vanishes and the crown is destroyed.

**Destruction** Aside from the summoning ability, it is destroyed if taken onto the Plane of Shadow.

### Council Helm

**Aura** strong abjuration; **CL** 20th; **Slot** head; **Weight** 3 lbs.

Members of a secretive administrative council retain their anonymity by wearing close-faced, magically empowered helmets that function as a *helm of telepathy* combined with an *amulet of proof against detection and location*. The wearer can use *disguise self* at will and benefits from *discern lies* and *true seeing* at all times. The helm provides 100% protection from all gaze attacks and all forms of magical control. Specific helms may have additional powers, and all are unique in appearance.

**Destruction** They function as mantles of office: if one helm is worn by five or more individuals in one 24-hour period, it ceases to function for 1 day; if worn by five or more individuals within 24 hours of regaining its properties, it permanently loses its magic.

### Hourglass of Shadows

**Aura** strong necromancy (evil); **CL** 17th; **Slot** none; **Weight** 350 lbs.

A foul 5-foot-tall device with a central chamber of etched, smoke-colored glass, suspended on an iron stand with pivots, that turns a creature's remains into an undead shadow. The user places the ashes of a Small, Medium or Large sentient creature into the chamber with a crushed onyx worth 100 gp. After the ashes flow through the chamber three times (3 hours), the contents can be emptied. If deposited into a clay pot filled with grave dirt and brackish water, the ashes transform the container into a **shadow urn**. A creature whose remains have been defiled this way cannot be restored to life by any method short of a *miracle* or *wish* until the shadow urn containing its spirit has been destroyed.

A shadow urn is fragile; it can be thrown (range increment 10 feet) and shatters on contact with any creature or solid surface, releasing the single greater shadow inside. This shadow is free-willed, owing no allegiance to the owner or whoever broke the urn, and typically attacks the nearest creature. A shadow urn is destroyed when used; if not shattered it holds the soul indefinitely.

**Destruction** Casting a spell with the light descriptor on it renders it inoperative for 1 day per spell level. Casting *sunburst* on it under the noonday sun shatters it. A shadow urn broken in full natural sunlight destroys the shadow within rather than releasing it.

### Id Portrait

**Aura** strong enchantment, necromancy and universal; **CL** 20th; **Slot** none; **Weight** 50 lbs.

This 4-foot-by-8-foot masterwork oil-on-wood painting depicts an elegant high-backed padded chair in a gothic study, in an etched silver-and-gold frame glowing with faint arcane symbols. It can create one or more duplicates of any living creature with a Charisma score of at least 10. By touching it and uttering a command word, the subject is drawn into the painting and appears seated in the chair, under the effects of *binding*. Delving into the subject's subconscious, the painting creates a duplicate that lasts 1 day per Hit Die the subject has. If the subject has a positive Charisma modifier, additional duplicates equal to that modifier may be created, each representing an alter ego of the subject; the subject decides how many, up to the maximum.

An id duplicate can be of a different class from the subject, has average hit points for that class, the same number of Hit Dice, and the same basic knowledge and goals (modified by ability score changes). It is an average member of its class with appropriate skills and feats; reallocate ability scores to suit the new class. It carries out whatever tasks the original desires. If all duplicates are destroyed, the original is ejected from the painting, permanently loses 1 point of Charisma (replaceable only by *miracle* or *wish*), and is under *slow* and *dimensional anchor* for the following 24 hours (cannot be dispelled). When a duplicate has lived out its time it simply dies; its body remains as a normal corpse and spells such as *miracle*, *raise dead* and *resurrection* fail on it. An id duplicate has a CR equal to the base creature's CR –1.

**Destruction** It can only be destroyed by painting a fresh portrait seated in the chair with marvelous pigments; the subject must be the original creator of the artifact.

### Warding Spike

**Aura** strong abjuration; **CL** 20th; **Slot** none; **Weight** 16 lbs.

An ancient item forged of cold iron with an adamantine core, designed to keep demons at bay. When driven into the wall of a building, it infuses the structure with a *dimensional lock* effect and a ward that repels evil outsiders. Any outsider with the evil subtype that tries to enter or damage the warded building must succeed at a DC 30 Will save or take 12d6 damage and be nauseated for 1 hour; one that stays inside makes a new save each round. Non-outsider evil creatures are also affected but gain a +4 bonus on Will saves, take only 4d6 on a failed save and are nauseated for only 1 minute. The stone and doors of the structure are magically treated (+20 to Break DCs, double hardness, double hit points) and self-repair 5 points of damage per round (only damage inflicted while the spike was functioning).

It consumes enormous magical energy: enough to ward a structure for 24 hours, after which it goes dormant until recharged. Recharging requires a spellcaster in contact for 10 minutes to expend spell slots totaling no fewer than 24 spell levels (prepared spells in these slots are lost for the day; spell-like abilities can't fuel it).

**Destruction** It must be hammered into a structure in the Abyss using a tool bearing the unholy symbol of any demon lord.

### Chitin Sabre

**Aura** strong evocation; **CL** 20th; **Slot** none; **Weight** 2 lbs.

### Warlord's Cursed Greatsword

A cursed intelligent +3 *wounding* greatsword. ### Phylactery of the Failed

**Aura** strong necromancy (evil); **CL** 18th; **Slot** none; **Weight** 5 lbs.

Not every lich succeeds in the transition to undeath. When a spellcaster attempts lichdom and fails, there is a minute chance (1 in 100) that the body is destroyed but the phylactery he prepared remains, invested with a remnant of his consciousness. The creature that opens a phylactery of the failed must immediately succeed at a DC 30 Will save or be affected by a disease-like curse called a **parasitic soul**. The victim's own personality and sanity are overwhelmed by the remnant ambitions and memories of the would-be lich. If the cursed creature is killed by the parasitic soul, her body animates 1 day later as a lich with the same memories and abilities as the magic-user who created the phylactery, and she cannot be returned to life until the lich is destroyed. If the parasitic soul is excised from a victim it returns to the phylactery and tries to infect the next creature to open the box. It infects only one creature at a time; while a creature is infected the phylactery has no dangerous properties.

*Parasitic Soul* Curse and disease; save Will DC 30; frequency 1/day; effect 1d6 Intelligence damage; cure a parasitic soul can only be cured by casting both a spell that prevents the target from being mentally controlled (like *protection from evil*) and *remove curse*.

**Destruction** Casting *resurrection* on a phylactery of the failed restores the would-be lich to life as a living spellcaster with the statistics he had before attempting the transformation. This destroys the phylactery but brings a powerful evil spellcaster back into being.

### Visionary Lens

**Aura** strong divination, enchantment and illusion; **CL** 20th; **Slot** none; **Weight** 50 lbs.

An ornate mirror 4 feet in diameter, framed in gold and silver, with a frame of stylized eyes that open and close. By day the mirror acts as a *crystal ball with telepathy*. At night the eyes can cast *scrying* (looking into the dreams of subjects) and *nightmare* instead. During the twilight hours between day and night, the eyes can bestow one of the following effects on any single person gazing into the mirror: *geas* (DC 21), *maze* (DC 22) or a *vision*. The mirror does not function outside of the plane of fey or demiplanes that share its characteristics.

**Destruction** The lens shatters if exposed to a *wail of the banshee* cast by someone wearing a *medallion of thought projection*.

### Warding Box

**Aura** strong abjuration; **CL** 20th; **Slot** none; **Weight** 5 lbs.

A powerful magical container built to obscure and confound any attempt to locate objects kept inside. The box has three compartments, each holding one object no larger than 8 inches by 8 inches by 3 inches (or up to 25 pounds). The lid and additional compartments are sealed, but the box can be opened or closed as a full-round action. While the lid is closed, the box and all objects inside are protected from all divination spells; no divination spell, save for those employed by the gods, can detect the box or its contents.

**Destruction** Place a *portable hole* inside it, close the lid, then place the box inside a second *portable hole*. Close the second hole, then use a *wish* to switch the two holes. This destroys both portable holes and the box.

### Vernal Key

**Aura** strong transmutation; **CL** 20th; **Slot** none; **Weight** 5 lbs.

Three lattice orbs, one inside the other, all carved from a single piece of blue mineral. Originally made to unlock a prison, as a side effect it protects the region around it from the ravages of winter. Within a 5-mile radius, otherwise cold weather (below 40° F) becomes moderate, while severe cold (below 0° F) becomes only cold. A mild wind marks the edge of the effect. The key must remain stationary (unattended) to produce this effect, and takes 24 hours to attune itself to its locale. It only ameliorates natural weather; it has no effect on magically created effects or extraplanar environments.

**Destruction** The key's power is tied to the prison it was made to open; destroying that prison destroys the key.
