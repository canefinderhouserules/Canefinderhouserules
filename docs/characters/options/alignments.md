# Alignments

**General rule:** your actions can change your alignment.

**Classes with alignment requirements:** these are enforced less drastically than the Core Rulebook, mainly in the matter of losing all class abilities or being forbidden to gain levels, though it depends on the case. Instead, straying from the required alignment generates [stress](stress-and-grace.md).

**Monk:** if a monk stops being lawful, he can keep gaining monk levels but gains stress: the further from lawful, the more stress. He is no longer so structured, and the mental burden of having to be structured in order to improve causes stress.

**Paladin:** if a paladin is only 1 step away from lawful good there is little drama (especially if the deity is not lawful good), but he may begin to gain stress. If he strays 2 steps from his deity's alignment, he loses all his powers. Why stress? Because being further and further from absolute, kindly righteousness fills him with doubts about that ideal, making him think he trained his whole life for nothing and that his god could strip him of his powers.

**Druid:** rather than going strictly by alignment, the GM looks at the character's ideals, though neutral is preferred. If a druid is not neutral, or stops being so, he must at least follow some ideal linked to nature in some way. If he does not follow it, begins to stop following it, or the original ideal he trained with was closely tied to his alignment, he begins to gain stress, to the point that if he no longer has the slightest respect for any natural law, he loses his powers.
