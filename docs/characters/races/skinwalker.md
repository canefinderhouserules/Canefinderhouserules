# Skinwalker

Skinwalkers are humanoids with a lycanthrope crouching somewhere in the branches of their family tree. Most people believe they are half-breeds of lycanthropes, or that they share some slight version of the curse. Skinwalker shamans say they were chosen by nature or the gods to be a bridge between the humanoid world and the animal world. A pair of humans may give birth to a skinwalker, the dormant lycanthropic ancestry making itself known in a child neither beast nor human, or a pair of skinwalker parents may produce a skinwalker child, though such couplings can sometimes produce human children as well.

**Physical Description**: Outwardly human, skinwalkers are virtually indistinguishable from others of the ethnic group into which they are born. Those rare skinwalkers who have bred true for generations tend to have dark eyes, straight dark hair and richly colored skin. Their features vary greatly when they shapechange. They gain a bestial visage evoking the creatures they emulate: the snout elongates, teeth become more prominent, claws emerge from fingertips and skin toughens into hide. Despite their shapechanging abilities, they are not immune to infection from full lycanthropes.

**Society**: Skinwalkers sometimes weave themselves into other societies, hoping their peculiar talents go unnoticed. They may also be nomads found in lands with wide-open spaces. Groups of skinwalkers hidden among a larger community of non-skinwalkers may regularly gather for lunar rituals or nocturnal pack hunting.

**Relations**: Skinwalkers mostly populate human settlements and communities. They tend to get along with catfolk, tengus and members of other animal-like races (including lycanthropes), and are comfortable taking their animalistic forms in the company of these fellows. However, many natural lycanthropes see skinwalkers as weaker, inferior versions of their own kind, and some lycanthropes even take to infecting or hunting their distant kin.

**Alignment and Religion**: Skinwalkers tend toward neutral alignments, but may be of any. Many venerate deities with close ties to nature and the animal world, as well as those connected with hunting, transformation, travel and trickery.

**Adventurers**: Often on the move, skinwalkers make for excellent adventurers. Once they become comfortable in a group, most skinwalkers come to see their adventuring allies as their pack or herd.

**Names**: Skinwalkers born in human and humanoid societies share the naming conventions of the surrounding culture. Skinwalkers born in their own packs typically have names similar to their overarching ethnicities appended with flavorful, animal-inspired appellations such as Bearmaw, Wolftamer, Fangblood or Owlmarked.

## Skinwalker Racial Traits (10 RP)

All skinwalkers are humanoids with the skinwalker and shapechanger subtypes. They have the same random starting age, aging effects, and random height and weight as a human.

**+2 Wisdom, –2 Intelligence, +2 to One Physical Ability Score While Shapechanged (1 RP)**: Skinwalkers are well attuned to the natural order of things and value passion above reason. While in their bestial form, they gain a +2 racial bonus to either Strength, Dexterity or Constitution (see change shape).

**Medium**: Skinwalkers are Medium creatures and have no bonuses or penalties due to their size.

**Normal Speed**: Skinwalkers have a base speed of 30 feet.

**Low-Light Vision**: In dim light, skinwalkers can see twice as far as humans.

**Animal-Minded (2 RP)**: Skinwalkers have a +2 racial bonus on Handle Animal and wild empathy checks.

**Change Shape (Su, 5 RP)**: A skinwalker can change shape into a bestial form as a standard action. In bestial form, a skinwalker gains a +2 racial bonus to either Strength, Dexterity or Constitution. While in this form, a skinwalker also takes on an animalistic feature that provides a special effect. Each time a skinwalker assumes bestial form, she can choose to gain one of the following features:

- 2 claw attacks that each deal 1d4 points of damage
- Darkvision to a range of 60 feet
- +1 racial bonus to natural armor

The racial ability score bonus and additional feature last as long as the skinwalker remains in that form, and a skinwalker can remain in bestial form for as long as she wants. While in bestial form, a skinwalker takes a –4 penalty on Charisma and Charisma-based checks when interacting with humanoids that lack the shapechanger subtype.

A skinwalker can return to her humanoid form as a swift action. To change forms and gain a different benefit, a skinwalker must first return to her humanoid form, then use her shapechange ability again. A skinwalker can shapechange into bestial form a number of times per day equal to 3 + 1/2 her character level. Different skinwalker heritages (see below) allow skinwalker characters to select from different sets of bestial features.

**Spell-Like Ability**: Skinwalkers with a Wisdom score of 11 or higher can use *speak with animals* once per day as a spell-like ability. The caster level for this ability is equal to the skinwalker's character level.

**Languages**: Skinwalkers begin play speaking Common. Skinwalkers with high Intelligence scores can choose from any language they want (other than secret languages).

## Non-Human Skinwalkers

Skinwalkers are found mostly in human populations. However, because of the nature of lycanthropy, skinwalkers can feasibly be of any intelligent humanoid race. A lycanthropic bloodline can descend through non-humans such as halflings, dwarves, orcs or elves. Non-human skinwalkers perhaps more closely resemble their non-lycanthropic parent race, but possess the same statistics as human-descended skinwalkers with the exception of size and effects dependent on size, such as the damage die of natural attacks. A gnome skinwalker, for example, is Small but otherwise statistically identical to the base skinwalker (unless she descends from an alternate heritage, in which case she has the statistics of that heritage). Skinwalkers of non-human descent are typically raised in the same culture as their parent race, and so usually have the same cultural understanding and education that anyone in their community possesses.

## Skinwalker Heritages

The base skinwalker is merely the most common of its kind. Some skinwalkers have inherited a larger share of their ancestors' animalistic nature, and show the traits of one specific type of lycanthrope. Eight alternative heritages can be selected during character creation; once chosen, a skinwalker's heritage cannot be changed. What a skinwalker of a specific heritage lacks in versatility, she makes up in the honed and specialized powers of her forebears.

A skinwalker of a specific heritage replaces or alters the following default racial traits:

- She gains bonuses and penalties to particular ability scores, but cannot select a different ability score to bolster each time she changes shape.
- She gains a different spell-like ability, tied to her heritage.
- She replaces the bonuses on Handle Animal and wild empathy checks with bonuses on other checks.
- She gains access to a different, larger set of bestial features when in bestial form. When she changes shape she chooses one feature from her heritage's list unless she has Extra Feature or a similar ability.

Where a heritage lists skill bonuses, the skinwalker gains a +2 racial bonus on each, in place of the Animal-Minded bonus.

### Werebat-Kin (Bloodmarked)

Bloodmarked are gregarious yet savage, communal and quick to defer to the leader of their flock, though their human blood lets them break from the group when they must. They are fond of elaborate ceremonies that bind a flock together, and they have a hunger for blood and a cruelly hedonistic streak. Bloodmarked who drift away from their kin usually build a surrogate flock out of a gang or a close-knit adventuring party. Their powers first show as a sudden sharpness of hearing, and most bloodmarked adventurers favor roguish pursuits, tactical fighting or wizardry. Full werebats are ruled by a dominant werebat master, who sometimes lets bloodmarked join the flock as lackeys able to move among human prey unnoticed.

**Ancestry**: werebat. **Typical Alignment**: NE.

**Ability Modifiers**: +2 Int, –2 Wis (+2 Dex while shapechanged).

**Skill Bonuses**: Fly, Perception at night.

**Spell-Like Ability**: obscuring mist 1/day.

**Bestial Features** (choose one each time you change shape):

- Bite attack that deals 1d6 points of damage
- +4 racial bonus on Perception checks
- Scent to a range of 30 feet
- Treat falling damage as 20 feet less than it actually is

### Werebear-Kin (Coldborn)

Coldborn are aware, disciplined and industrious, with a violent temper hidden beneath a solitary, good-hearted exterior. They often become torpid and withdrawn in winter unless they have matters to attend to. They avoid frequent dealings with outsiders but fiercely protect their chosen family, which makes them effective leaders for tight-knit adventuring parties. Most coldborn adventurers are druids, rangers, witches or monks. Their first burst of strength usually comes during or shortly after puberty, often as an accident that breaks something. Full werebears are protectors of the wilds who associate closely with druids and clerics of nature deities.

**Ancestry**: werebear. **Typical Alignment**: LG.

**Ability Modifiers**: +2 Con, –2 Cha (+2 Wis while shapechanged).

**Skill Bonuses**: Climb, wild empathy.

**Spell-Like Ability**: calm animals 1/day.

**Bestial Features** (choose one each time you change shape):

- Bite attack that deals 1d6 points of damage
- 2 claw attacks that each deal 1d4 points of damage
- Climb speed of 20 feet
- Scent to a range of 30 feet

### Wereboar-Kin (Ragebred)

Ragebred are known for their fiery tempers, stubborn streaks, strong familial bonds and tempestuous romances. Their first transformation is usually triggered by an injury or a reckless burst of adrenaline, and many struggle to keep their emotions, and so their powers, in check. They are often ashamed of their heritage and scorned for it. Most ragebred adventurers are barbarians, fighters or clerics of war and might. Full wereboars are thuggish, domineering and even more ashamed of their condition than their kin.

**Ancestry**: wereboar. **Typical Alignment**: CN.

**Ability Modifiers**: +2 Wis, –2 Cha (+2 Con while shapechanged).

**Skill Bonuses**: Handle Animal, Perception.

**Spell-Like Ability**: speak with animals (pigs and boars only) 3/day.

**Bestial Features** (choose one each time you change shape):

- +10-foot racial bonus to base speed
- Gore attack that deals 1d6 points of damage
- 2 hoof attacks that each deal 1d4 points of damage
- Scent to a range of 30 feet

### Werecrocodile-Kin (Scaleheart)

Scalehearts are cold, patient and ruthless. Their culture respects nothing so much as dominance, shown physically, through cunning or through magic, and many have a hard time reading or expressing emotion. They dwell in low buildings by the water, and herbalism, alchemy and witchcraft are respected trades among them. Their first transformation is often triggered by the first serious injury they witness or a sudden hunger. Full werecrocodiles lure victims far from help before hunting them down, and think little of imposing their will on scalehearts.

**Ancestry**: werecrocodile. **Typical Alignment**: NE.

**Ability Modifiers**: +2 Int, –2 Wis (+2 Str while shapechanged).

**Skill Bonuses**: Stealth, wild empathy.

**Spell-Like Ability**: scare 1/day.

**Bestial Features** (choose one each time you change shape):

- Bite attack that deals 1d6 points of damage
- Darkvision to a range of 60 feet
- Swim speed of 30 feet
- Ferocity (the creature remains conscious and can keep fighting below 0 hit points; it is still staggered, loses 1 hit point each round, and dies when its hit points reach a negative amount equal to its Constitution score)

### Wererat-Kin (Nightskulk)

Nightskulks are short, wiry and watchful, and they have learned to hide among the human masses of large cities. They take most of their culture from the society around them and are often despised for the disease and crime their kin are blamed for. They form long-lasting bonds with a few close friends and are driven to build a secure hideout furnished with precious possessions. Most nightskulk adventurers are rogues, with a few bards and monks. Full wererats are insular and cowardly, preferring strength in numbers and the sewers over the open street.

**Ancestry**: wererat. **Typical Alignment**: LE.

**Ability Modifiers**: +2 Int, –2 Str (+2 Dex while shapechanged).

**Skill Bonuses**: Handle Animal, Stealth in urban environments.

**Spell-Like Ability**: speak with animals (rodents only) 3/day.

**Bestial Features** (choose one each time you change shape):

- Bite attack that deals 1d6 points of damage
- Climb speed of 30 feet
- Distraction (as the swarm ability; any living creature that begins its turn in the skinwalker's space must succeed at a Fortitude save, DC 10 + 1/2 the skinwalker's character level + her Constitution modifier, or be nauseated for 1 round)
- Scent to a range of 30 feet

### Wereshark-Kin (Seascarred)

Seascarred are single-minded, blunt and strong-willed, and many think of themselves as sea nobility. They proudly display large scars or tattoos as proof of their strength. Some guard a stretch of coast under the guidance of a druid or shaman, while the selfish and the unscrupulous turn to piracy. Their first transformation is usually in or near the sea and brings an overwhelming awareness of all the life around them. Most become clerics of nature deities, druids, oracles, fighters, rangers or magi. Full weresharks rule island communities and recruit true sharks to enforce their dominance.

**Ancestry**: wereshark. **Typical Alignment**: N.

**Ability Modifiers**: +2 Wis, –2 Int (+2 Con while shapechanged).

**Skill Bonuses**: Perception, Swim.

**Spell-Like Ability**: charm animal (aquatic animals only) 3/day.

**Bestial Features** (choose one each time you change shape):

- Amphibious
- Bite attack that deals 1d6 points of damage
- Ferocity (see werecrocodile-kin)
- Swim speed of 30 feet

### Weretiger-Kin (Fanglord)

Fanglords are proud, charming and intimidating, with an aristocratic bearing and unfettered attitudes. They live by intimidation, cutthroat business, political ploys and military ventures, and they make impeccable assassins and frustrating political enemies. They are patient, striking only when they can act with overwhelming effectiveness, and rarely loyal to anyone. Their first transformation is often triggered by a first romantic encounter. Full weretigers are egomaniacs with a talent for controlling others through fear.

**Ancestry**: weretiger. **Typical Alignment**: NE.

**Ability Modifiers**: +2 Dex, –2 Wis (+2 Cha while shapechanged).

**Skill Bonuses**: Acrobatics, Perception.

**Spell-Like Ability**: jump 1/day.

**Bestial Features** (choose one each time you change shape):

- Bite attack that deals 1d6 points of damage
- 2 claw attacks that each deal 1d4 points of damage
- +10-foot racial bonus to base speed
- See in darkness (the creature can see perfectly in darkness of any kind, including magical darkness)

### Werewolf-Kin (Witchwolf)

Witchwolves are passionate and intimidating, and often live in small semi-nomadic bands of extended families as traders, mystics, adventurers or entertainers. Their packs are frequently led by witches versed in curses and transformation. Many are savage and happy to rob or murder travelers, but a rare few fight against the image of werewolves as violent monsters and form cabals that defend an area from fiends, evil lycanthropes and other monsters. Their first transformation commonly occurs during puberty beneath a full moon. Some witchwolf bands train as monks, who developed the wolf style feats. Full werewolves hunt in packs under a pack lord, and the few that adventure often fight a losing battle against their own impulses.

**Ancestry**: werewolf. **Typical Alignment**: CE.

**Ability Modifiers**: +2 Con, –2 Int (+2 Wis while shapechanged).

**Skill Bonuses**: Perception, wild empathy.

**Spell-Like Ability**: magic fang 1/day.

**Bestial Features** (choose one each time you change shape):

- Bite attack that deals 1d6 points of damage
- 2 claw attacks that each deal 1d4 points of damage
- Darkvision to a range of 60 feet
- +2 racial bonus on all saving throws
