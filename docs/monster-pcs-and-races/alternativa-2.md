##### Energy Drain (Su)

A succubus drains energy from a mortal she lures into an act of passion, such as a kiss. An unwilling victim must be grappled before the succubus can use this ability. The succubus’s kiss bestows one negative level. The kiss also has the effect of a [*<u>suggestion</u>*](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic/all-spells/s/suggestion) spell, asking the victim to accept another act of passion from the succubus. The victim must succeed on a Will save to negate the [*<u>suggestion</u>*](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic/all-spells/s/suggestion). The DC is 10 + 1/2 level + charisma for the [<u>Fortitude</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat/#TOC-Fortitude) save to remove a negative level and the suggestion.

##### Profane Gift (Su)

Once per day as a full-round action, a succubus may grant a profane gift to a willing humanoid creature by touching it for 1 full round. The target gains a +2 profane bonus to an ability score of his choice. A single creature may have no more than one profane gift from a succubus at a time. As long as the profane gift persists, the succubus can communicate telepathically with the target across any distance (and may use her [*<u>suggestion</u>*](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic/all-spells/s/suggestion) spell-like ability through it). A profane gift is removed by [*<u>dispel evil</u>*](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic/all-spells/d/dispel-evil) or [*<u>dispel chaos</u>*](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic/all-spells/d/dispel-chaos). The succubus can remove it as well as a free action (causing 2d6 [<u>Charisma</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores/#Charisma_Cha) [<u>drain</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/bestiary/rules-for-monsters/universal-monster-rules#TOC-Ability-Damage-and-Drain-Ex-or-Su-) to the victim, no save).

Cambions excel at causing pain and anguish. ##### Sinfrenzy (Su)

Every cambion carries an excessive capacity for one of the seven classical sins, determined at the moment of the cambion’s birth and depending on the nature of his [<u>humanoid</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/bestiary/rules-for-monsters/creature-types#TOC-Humanoid) parent’s greatest sin. Once per day for a number of rounds equal to his [<u>Hit Dice</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/glossary#TOC-Hit-Dice-HD-), a [<u>cambion</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/bestiary/monster-listings/outsiders/demon/cambion) can embrace his sin and enter a frenzied state as a [<u>free action</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Free-Actions). While a [<u>cambion</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/bestiary/monster-listings/outsiders/demon/cambion) is in this frenzy, his land speed increases by 10 feet and he gains a +1 bonus on [<u>Reflex</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Reflex) saves and [<u>attack rolls</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Attack-Roll). These bonuses do not stack with those granted by [*<u>haste</u>*](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic/all-spells/h/haste) or similar effects, but if the cambion has at least 1 level in the class listed for his specific sin, the sinfrenzy grants an additional effect.

When they are in a sinfrenzy, their [<u>channel energy</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/classes/core-classes/cleric#TOC-Channel-Energy-Su-) effect increases by 1d6.

- **Gluttony ([<u>alchemist</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/classes/base-classes/alchemist))**: Cambion [<u>alchemists</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/classes/base-classes/alchemist) enjoy drinking extracts, [*<u>potions</u>*](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic-items/potions), mutagens, and even poisons. When they are in a sinfrenzy, their bombs deal an additional die of damage.

- **Greed ([<u>rogue</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/classes/core-classes/rogue))**: Cambion [<u>rogues</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/classes/core-classes/rogue) are obsessed with gathering wealth and power. When they are in a sinfrenzy, [<u>cambion</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/bestiary/monster-listings/outsiders/demon/cambion) [<u>rogues</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/classes/core-classes/rogue) deal an additional die of [<u>sneak attack</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/classes/core-classes/rogue#TOC-Sneak-Attack) damage.

- **Lust ([<u>bard</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/classes/core-classes/bard))**: Cambion [<u>bards</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/classes/core-classes/bard) seek to dominate and control others, turning them into slaves. - **Pride ([<u>wizard</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/classes/core-classes/wizard))**: Cambion [<u>wizards</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/classes/core-classes/wizard) believe their magic is the greatest. When they are in a sinfrenzy, their spell save DCs increase by 1.

- **Sloth ([<u>fighter</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/classes/core-classes/fighter))**: Cambion [<u>fighters</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/classes/core-classes/fighter) [<u>bleed</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/conditions#TOC-Bleed) slowly. ##### Bleed

A creature that is taking bleed damage takes the listed amount of damage at the beginning of its turn. Bleeding can be stopped by a DC 20 [<u>Heal</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/skills/heal) check or through the application of any spell that cures hit point damage (even if the bleed is ability damage). Some bleed effects cause ability damage or even ability drain. Bleed Damage effects stack with each other, Bleed ability damage and drain don’t.

##### Blinded

The creature cannot see. It takes a –2 penalty to [<u>Armor Class</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Armor-Class), loses its [<u>Dexterity</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores#TOC-Dexterity-Dex-) bonus to [<u>AC</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Armor-Class) (if any), and takes a –4 penalty on most [<u>Strength</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores#TOC-Strength-Str-)– and [<u>Dexterity</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores#TOC-Dexterity-Dex-)-based skill checks and on opposed [<u>Perception</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/skills/perception) skill checks. All checks and activities that rely on vision (such as reading and [<u>Perception</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/skills/perception) checks based on sight) automatically fail. All opponents are considered to have total [<u>concealment</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Concealment) (30% miss chance) against the blinded character. Blind creatures must make a DC 10 [<u>Acrobatics</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/skills/acrobatics) skill check to move faster than half speed. Creatures that fail this check fall [<u>prone</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Prone). Characters who remain blinded for a long time grow accustomed to these drawbacks and can overcome some of them.

##### Broken

Items that have taken damage in excess of half their total [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points) gain the broken condition, meaning they are less effective at their designated task. The broken condition has the following effects, depending upon the item.

- If the item is a weapon, any attacks made with the item suffer a –2 penalty on attack and damage rolls. Such weapons only score a critical hit on a natural 20 and only deal ×2 damage on a confirmed critical hit.

- If the item is a suit of armor or a shield, the bonus it grants to [<u>AC</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Armor-Class) is halved, rounding down. Broken armor doubles its armor check penalty on skills.

- If the item is a tool needed for a skill, any skill check made with the item takes a –2 penalty.

- If the item is a [<u>wand</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic-items/wands) or staff, it uses up twice as many charges when used.

- If the item does not fit into any of these categories, the broken condition has no effect on its use. Items with the broken condition, regardless of type, are worth 75% of their normal value. If the item is magical, it can only be repaired with a [*<u>mending</u>*](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic/all-spells/m/mending) or [*<u>make whole</u>*](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic/all-spells/m/make-whole) spell cast by a character with a [<u>caster level</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic#TOC-Caster-Level) equal to or higher than the item’s. Items lose the broken condition if the spell restores the object to half its original [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points) or higher. Non-magical items can be repaired in a similar fashion, or through the [<u>Craft</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/skills/craft) skill used to create it. Generally speaking, this requires a DC 20 [<u>Craft</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/skills/craft) check and 1 hour of work to be repaired. Most craftsmen charge one-tenth the item’s total cost to repair such damage (more if the item is badly damaged or ruined).

**Special Note on Ships**: Ships, and sometimes their means of propulsion—are objects, and like any other object, when they take damage in excess of half their [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points), they gain the broken condition. When a ship gains the broken condition, it takes a –2 penalty to AC, on sailing checks, saving throws, and on [<u>combat maneuver</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Combat-Maneuvers) checks. If a ship or its means of propulsion becomes broken, the ship’s maximum speed is halved and the ship can no longer gain the upper hand until repaired. If the ship is in motion and traveling faster than its new maximum speed, it automatically decelerates to its new maximum speed (from [<u>Advanced Naval Combat</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/other-rules/ship-combat/advanced-naval-combat).)

##### Confused

A confused creature is mentally befuddled and cannot act normally. A confused creature cannot tell the difference between ally and foe, treating all creatures as enemies. Allies wishing to cast a beneficial spell that requires a touch on a confused creature must succeed on a melee [<u>touch attack</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Touch-Attacks). If a confused creature is attacked, it attacks the creature that last attacked it until that creature is [<u>dead</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Dead) or out of sight.

Roll on the following table at the beginning of each confused subject’s turn each round to see what the subject does in that round.

| **d%** | **Behavior**                                                                                 |
|--------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 01–40  | Act normally.                                                                                |
| 41–60  | Do nothing but babble incoherently.                                                          |
| 61–75  | Deal 1d8 points of damage + Str modifier to self with item in hand.                          |
| 76–100 | Attack nearest creature (for this purpose, a familiar counts as part of the subject’s self). |

A confused creature who can’t carry out the indicated action does nothing but babble incoherently. Attackers are not at any special advantage when attacking a confused creature. Any confused creature who is attacked automatically attacks its attackers on its next turn, as long as it is still confused when its turn comes. Note that a confused creature will not make attacks of opportunity against anything that it is not already devoted to attacking (either because of its most recent action or because it has just been attacked).

##### Dead

The character’s [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points) are reduced to a negative amount equal to his [<u>Constitution</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores#TOC-Constitution-Con-) score, his [<u>Constitution</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores#TOC-Constitution-Con-) drops to 0, or he is killed outright by a spell or effect. The character’s soul leaves his body. Dead characters cannot benefit from normal or magical healing, but they can be restored to life via magic. A [<u>dead</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Dead) body decays normally unless magically preserved, but magic that restores a [<u>dead</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Dead) character to life also restores the body either to full health or to its condition at the time of death (depending on the spell or device). Either way, resurrected characters need not worry about rigor mortis, decomposition, and other conditions that affect dead bodies.

##### Deafened

A deafened character cannot hear. He takes a –4 penalty on [<u>initiative</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Initiative) checks, automatically fails [<u>Perception</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/skills/perception) checks based on sound, takes a –4 penalty on opposed [<u>Perception</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/skills/perception) checks, and has a 40% chance of spell failure when casting spells with verbal [<u>components</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic#TOC-Components). Characters who remain deafened for a long time grow accustomed to these drawbacks and can overcome some of them.

##### Disabled

A character with 0 [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points), or one who has negative [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points) but has become [<u>stable</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Stable) and conscious, is disabled. A disabled character may take a single [<u>move action</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Move-Actions) or [<u>standard action</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Standard-Actions) each round (but not both, nor can he take full-round actions, but he can still take swift, immediate, and free actions). He moves at half speed. Taking move actions doesn’t risk further injury, but performing any [<u>standard action</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Standard-Actions) (or any other action the GM deems strenuous, including some free actions such as casting a [<u>Quicken Spell</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/feats/metamagic-feats/quicken-spell-metamagic) spell) deals 1 point of damage after the completion of the act. Unless the action increased the disabled character’s [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points), he is now in negative [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points) and [<u>dying</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Dying).

A disabled character with negative [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points) recovers [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points) naturally if he is being helped. Otherwise, each day he can attempt a DC 10 [<u>Constitution</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores#TOC-Constitution-Con-) check after resting for 8 hours, to begin recovering [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points) naturally. The character takes a penalty on this roll equal to his negative hit point total. Failing this check causes the character to lose 1 hit point, but this does not cause the character to become [<u>unconscious</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Unconscious). Once a character makes this check, he continues to heal naturally and is no longer in danger of losing [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points) naturally.

##### Dying

A dying creature is [<u>unconscious</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Unconscious) and near death. Creatures that have negative [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points) and have not stabilized are dying. A dying creature can take no actions. On the character’s next turn, after being reduced to negative [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points) (but not [<u>dead</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Dead)), and on all subsequent turns, the character must make a DC 10 [<u>Constitution</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores#TOC-Constitution-Con-) check to become [<u>stable</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Stable). The character takes a penalty on this roll equal to his negative hit point total. A character that is [<u>stable</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Stable) does not need to make this check. A natural 20 on this check is an automatic success. If the character fails this check, he loses 1 hit point. If a dying creature has an amount of negative [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points) equal to its [<u>Constitution</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores#TOC-Constitution-Con-) score, it dies.

##### Energy Drained

The character gains one or more negative levels, which might become permanent. If the subject has at least as many negative levels as Hit Dice, he dies.

Some spells and a number of [<u>undead</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/bestiary/rules-for-monsters/creature-types/#Undead) creatures have the ability to drain away life and energy; this dreadful attack results in “negative levels.” These cause a character to take a number of penalties.

For each negative level a creature has, it takes a cumulative –1 penalty on all ability checks, [<u>attack rolls</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat/#Attack_Roll), [<u>combat maneuver</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat/#Combat_Maneuvers) checks, [<u>Combat Maneuver Defense</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat/#Combat_Maneuver_Defense), saving throws, and skill checks. In addition, the creature reduces its current and total [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/glossary/#Hit_Points_hp) by 5 for each negative level it possesses. The creature is also treated as one level lower for the purpose of level-dependent variables (such as spellcasting) for each negative level possessed. Spellcasters do not lose any prepared spells or slots as a result of negative levels. If a creature’s negative levels equal or exceed its total [<u>Hit Dice</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/glossary/#Hit_Dice_HD), it dies.

A creature with temporary negative levels receives a new saving throw to remove the negative level each day. The DC of this save is the same as the effect that caused the negative levels.

Some abilities and spells (such as [*<u>raise dead</u>*](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic/all-spells/r/raise-dead/)) bestow permanent level drain on a creature. These are treated just like temporary negative levels, but they do not allow a new save each day to remove them. Level drain can be removed through spells like [*<u>restoration</u>*](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic/all-spells/r/restoration/). Permanent negative levels remain after a [<u>dead</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/conditions/#Dead) creature is restored to life. A creature whose permanent negative levels equal its [<u>Hit Dice</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/glossary/#Hit_Dice_HD) cannot be brought back to life through spells like [*<u>raise dead</u>*](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic/all-spells/r/raise-dead/) and [*<u>resurrection</u>*](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic/all-spells/r/resurrection/) without also receiving a [*<u>restoration</u>*](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic/all-spells/r/restoration/) spell, cast the round after it is restored to life.

##### Entangled

The character is ensnared. Being entangled impedes movement, but does not entirely prevent it unless the bonds are anchored to an immobile object or tethered by an opposing force. An entangled creature moves at half speed, cannot run or [<u>charge</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Charge), and takes a –2 penalty on all attack rolls and a –4 penalty to [<u>Dexterity</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores#TOC-Dexterity-Dex-). An [<u>entangled</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Entangled) character who attempts to cast a spell must make a [<u>concentration</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic#TOC-Concentration) check (DC 15 + spell level) or lose the spell.  
  
Una move te permite repetir la save o tirar un escape artist o str check vs el DC original que lo provoco (si no lo provoco algo con DC, el DC se vuelve 10 + nivel del personaje).

##### Exhausted

An exhausted character moves at half speed, cannot run or [<u>charge</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Charge), and takes a –6 penalty to [<u>Strength</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores#TOC-Strength-Str-) and [<u>Dexterity</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores#TOC-Dexterity-Dex-). After 1 hour of complete rest, an exhausted character becomes fatigued. A fatigued character becomes exhausted by doing something else that would normally cause [<u>fatigue</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Fatigued).

##### Fascinated

A fascinated creature is entranced by a supernatural or spell effect. The creature stands or sits quietly, taking no actions other than to pay attention to the fascinating effect, for as long as the effect lasts. It takes a –4 penalty on skill checks made as reactions, such as [<u>Perception</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/skills/perception) checks. Any potential threat, such as a hostile creature approaching, allows the fascinated creature a new [<u>saving throw</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Saving-Throws) against the fascinating effect. Any obvious threat, such as someone drawing a weapon, casting a spell, or aiming a ranged weapon at the fascinated creature, automatically breaks the effect. A fascinated creature’s ally may shake it free of the spell as a [<u>standard action</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Standard-Actions).

##### Fatigued

A fatigued character can neither [<u>run</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Run) nor [<u>charge</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Charge) and takes a –2 penalty to [<u>Strength</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores#TOC-Strength-Str-) and [<u>Dexterity</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores#TOC-Dexterity-Dex-). Doing anything that would normally cause fatigue causes the fatigued character to become exhausted. After 8 hours of complete rest, fatigued characters are no longer fatigued.

##### Flat-Footed

A character who has not yet acted during a combat is flat-footed, unable to react normally to the situation. A flat-footed character loses his [<u>Dexterity</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores#TOC-Dexterity-Dex-) bonus to [<u>AC</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Armor-Class) and Combat Maneuver Defense (CMD) (if any) and cannot make attacks of opportunity, unless he has the [<u>Combat Reflexes</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/feats/combat-feats/combat-reflexes-combat) feat or Uncanny Dodge class ability.

Characters with Uncanny Dodge retain their [<u>Dexterity</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores#TOC-Dexterity-Dex-) bonus to their [<u>AC</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Armor-Class) and can make attacks of opportunity before they have acted in the first round of combat.

You cannot take immediate actions while flat-footed.

##### Grappled

A grappled creature is restrained by a creature, trap, or effect. Grappled creatures cannot move and take a –4 penalty to [<u>Dexterity</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores/#Dexterity_Dex). A grappled creature takes a –2 penalty on all attack rolls and combat maneuver checks, except those made to grapple or escape a grapple. In addition, grappled creatures can take no action that requires two hands to perform. A grappled character who attempts to cast a spell or use a spell-like ability must make a [<u>concentration</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic/#TOC-Concentration) check (DC 10 + grappler’s CMB + spell level), or lose the spell. Grappled creatures cannot make attacks of opportunity.

A grappled creature cannot use [<u>Stealth</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/skills/stealth/) to hide from the creature grappling it, even if a special ability, such as hide in plain sight, would normally allow it to do so. If a grappled creature becomes [<u>invisible</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Invisible), through a spell or other ability, it gains a +2 [<u>circumstance bonus</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/glossary/#TOC-Circumstance-Bonus) on its CMD to avoid being grappled, but receives no other benefit.

##### Helpless

A helpless character is [<u>paralyzed</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Paralyzed), held, bound, sleeping, [<u>unconscious</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Unconscious), or otherwise completely at an opponent’s mercy. A helpless target is treated as having a [<u>Dexterity</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores#TOC-Dexterity-Dex-) of 0 (–5 modifier). Melee attacks against a [<u>helpless</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Helpless) target get a +4 bonus (equivalent to attacking a [<u>prone</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Prone) target). Ranged attacks get no special bonus against [<u>helpless</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Helpless) targets. Rogues can sneak attack [<u>helpless</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Helpless) targets.

As a [<u>full-round action</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Full-Round-Actions), an enemy can use a melee weapon to deliver a coup de grace to a helpless foe. An enemy can also use a bow or crossbow, provided he is adjacent to the target. The attacker automatically hits and scores a critical hit. (A [<u>rogue</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/classes/core-classes/rogue) also gets his sneak attack damage bonus against a helpless foe when delivering a coup de grace.)Delivering a coup de grace provokes attacks of opportunity.

Creatures that are immune to critical hits do not take critical damage, nor do they need to make [<u>Fortitude</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Fortitude) saves to avoid being killed by a coup de grace.

##### Incorporeal

Creatures with the incorporeal condition do not have a physical body. Incorporeal creatures are immune to all nonmagical attack forms. Incorporeal creatures take half damage (50%) from magic weapons, spells, spell-like effects, and supernatural effects. Incorporeal creatures take full damage from other incorporeal creatures and effects, as well as all force effects. See [<u>here</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/bestiary/rules-for-monsters/universal-monster-rules/#Incorporeal_Ex) for additional information.

##### Invisible

Invisible creatures are visually undetectable. An invisible creature gains a +2 bonus on attack rolls against sighted opponents, and ignores its opponents’ [<u>Dexterity</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores#TOC-Dexterity-Dex-) bonuses to [<u>AC</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Armor-Class) (if any).

The ability to move about unseen is not foolproof. While they can’t be seen, invisible creatures can be heard, smelled, or felt.

Invisibility makes a creature undetectable by vision, including [<u>darkvision</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/Gamemastering/special-abilities/#Darkvision).

Invisibility does not, by itself, make a creature immune to critical hits, but it does make the creature immune to extra damage from being a [<u>ranger’s</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/classes/core-classes/ranger/) [<u>favored enemy</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/classes/core-classes/ranger/#Favored_Enemy_Ex) and from [<u>sneak attacks</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/classes/core-classes/rogue/#Sneak_Attack).

A creature can generally notice the presence of an active invisible creature within 30 feet with a DC 20 [<u>Perception</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/skills/perception/) check. The observer gains a hunch that “something’s there” but can’t see it or target it accurately with an attack. It’s practically impossible (+20 DC) to pinpoint an invisible creature’s location with a [<u>Perception</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/skills/perception/) check. Even once a character has pinpointed the square that contains an invisible creature, the creature still benefits from total [<u>concealment</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat/#Concealment) (30% miss chance). There are a number of modifiers that can be applied to this DC if the invisible creature is moving or engaged in a noisy activity.

A creature can grope about to find an invisible creature. A character can make a [<u>touch attack</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat/#Touch_Attacks) with his hands or a weapon into two adjacent 5-foot squares using a [<u>standard action</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/gamemastering/combat/#Standard_Action). If an invisible target is in the designated area, there is a 30% miss chance on the [<u>touch attack</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat/#Touch_Attacks). If successful, the groping character deals no damage but has successfully pinpointed the invisible creature’s current location. If the invisible creature moves, its location, obviously, is once again unknown.

If an invisible creature strikes a character, the character struck knows the location of the creature that struck him (until, of course, the invisible creature moves). The only exception is if the invisible creature has a reach greater than 5 feet. In this case, the struck character knows the general location of the creature but has not pinpointed the exact location.

If a character tries to attack an invisible creature whose location he has pinpointed, he attacks normally, but the invisible creature still benefits from full [<u>concealment</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat/#Concealment) (and thus a 30% miss chance). A particularly large and slow invisible creature might get a smaller miss chance.

If an invisible character picks up a visible object, the object remains visible. An invisible creature can pick up a small visible item and hide it on his person (tucked in a pocket or behind a cloak) and render it effectively invisible. One could coat an invisible object with flour to at least keep track of its position (until the flour falls off or blows away).

Invisible creatures leave tracks. They can be tracked normally. Footprints in sand, mud, or other soft surfaces can give enemies clues to an invisible creature’s location.

An invisible creature in the water displaces water, revealing its location. The invisible creature, however, is still hard to see and benefits from [<u>concealment</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat/#Concealment).

A creature with the [<u>scent</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/Gamemastering/special-abilities/#Scent) ability can detect an invisible creature as it would a visible one.

A creature with the [<u>Blind-Fight</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/feats/combat-feats/blind-fight-combat/) feat has a better chance to hit an invisible creature. Roll the miss chance twice, and he misses only if both rolls indicate a miss. (Alternatively, make one 25% miss chance roll rather than two 50% miss chance rolls.)

A creature with [<u>blindsight</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/Gamemastering/special-abilities/#Blindsight_and_Blindsense) can attack (and otherwise interact with) creatures regardless of invisibility.

An invisible burning torch still gives off light, as does an invisible object with a [*<u>light</u>*](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic/all-spells/l/light/) or similar spell cast upon it.

Ethereal creatures are invisible. Since ethereal creatures are not materially present, [<u>Perception</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/skills/perception/) checks, [<u>scent</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/Gamemastering/special-abilities/#Scent), [<u>Blind-Fight</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/feats/combat-feats/blind-fight-combat/), and [<u>blindsight</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/Gamemastering/special-abilities/#Blindsight_and_Blindsense) don’t help locate them.

[<u>Incorporeal</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/bestiary/rules-for-monsters/universal-monster-rules/#Incorporeal_Ex) creatures are often invisible. [<u>Scent</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/Gamemastering/special-abilities/#Scent), [<u>Blind-Fight</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/feats/combat-feats/blind-fight-combat/), and [<u>blindsight</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/Gamemastering/special-abilities/#Blindsight_and_Blindsense) don’t help creatures find or attack invisible, [<u>incorporeal</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/bestiary/rules-for-monsters/creature-types/#Incorporeal) creatures, but [<u>Perception</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/skills/perception/) checks can help.

Invisible creatures cannot use [<u>gaze</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/bestiary/rules-for-monsters/universal-monster-rules/#Gaze_Su) attacks.

Invisibility does not thwart [<u>divination</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic/#Divination) spells.

Since some creatures can detect or even see invisible creatures, it is helpful to be able to hide even when invisible.

##### Petrified 1\|2

Si te provocan petrified otra vez, pasas de 1 a 2.

A petrified 2 character has been turned to stone and is considered [<u>unconscious</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Unconscious). If a petrified character cracks or breaks, but the broken pieces are joined with the body as he returns to flesh, he is unharmed. If the character’s petrified body is incomplete when it returns to flesh, the body is likewise incomplete and there is some amount of permanent hit point loss and/or debilitation.

##### Pinned

A pinned creature is tightly bound and can take few actions. A pinned creature cannot move and is denied its Dexterity bonus. A pinned character also takes an additional –4 penalty to his [<u>Armor Class</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Armor-Class). A pinned creature is limited in the actions that it can take. A pinned creature can always attempt to free itself, usually through a combat maneuver check or [<u>Escape Artist</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/skills/escape-artist) check. A pinned creature can take verbal and mental actions, but cannot cast any spells that require a somatic or material component. A pinned character who attempts to cast a spell or use a spell-like ability must make a [<u>concentration</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic#TOC-Concentration) check (DC 10 + grappler’s CMB + spell level) or lose the spell. Pinned is a more severe version of [<u>grappled</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Grappled), and their effects do not stack.

##### Prone

The character is lying on the ground. A prone attacker has a –4 penalty on melee attack rolls and cannot use a ranged weapon (except for a crossbow). A prone defender gains a +4 bonus to [<u>Armor Class</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Armor-Class) against ranged attacks, but takes a –4 penalty to [<u>AC</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Armor-Class) against melee attacks.

Standing up is a move-equivalent action that provokes an attack of opportunity.

##### Shaken\|Frightened\|Panicked\|Cowering

A shaken character takes a –2 penalty on attack rolls, [<u>saving throws</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Saving-Throws), skill checks, and ability checks. Shaken is a less severe state of fear than [<u>frightened</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Frightened) or [<u>panicked</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Panicked).  
Shaken no prohibe castear con componentes psiquicos.

A Frightened character takes a –4 penalty on attack rolls, [<u>saving throws</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Saving-Throws), skill checks, and ability checks. Shaken is a less severe state of fear than [<u>frightened</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Frightened) or [<u>panicked</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Panicked).  
Frightened si prohibe castear con componentes psiquicos.

A panicked creature flees from the source of its fear as best it can. If unable to flee, it may fight. A panicked creature takes a –4 penalty on all attack rolls, [<u>saving throws</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Saving-Throws), skill checks, and ability checks. A frightened creature can use special abilities, including spells, to flee; indeed, the creature must use such means if they are the only way to escape.

panicked is like frightened, except that the creature must flee if possible.

A Cowering creature is frozen in fear and can take no actions. A cowering character takes a –2 penalty to [<u>Armor Class</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Armor-Class) and loses his [<u>Dexterity</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores#TOC-Dexterity-Dex-) bonus (if any).

##### Sickened

The character takes a –2 penalty on all attack rolls, weapon damage rolls, [<u>saving throws</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Saving-Throws), skill checks, and ability checks.

##### Sinking

A ship that is reduced to 0 or fewer [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points) gains the sinking condition. A sinking ship cannot move or attack, and it sinks completely 10 rounds after it gains the sinking condition. Each additional hit on a sinking ship that deals more than 25 points of damage reduces the remaining time for it to sink by 1 round. A ship that sinks completely drops to the bottom of the body of water and is considered destroyed. A destroyed ship cannot be repaired—it is so significantly damaged it cannot even be used for scrap material. Magic (such as [*<u>make whole</u>*](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/magic/all-spells/m/make-whole)) can repair a sinking ship if the ship’s [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points) are raised above 0, at which point the ship loses the sinking condition. Generally, non-magical repairs take too long to save a ship from sinking once it begins to go down.

##### Sleep 1\|2

Sleep 1 te deja la move o la standard, y te deja -2 a todas las tiradas y daño.

Sleep 2 te deja Unconscious.

##### Stable

A character who was [<u>dying</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Dying) but who has stopped losing [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points) each round and still has negative [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points) is stable. The character is no longer [<u>dying</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Dying), but is still [<u>unconscious</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Unconscious). If the character has become stable because of aid from another character (such as a [<u>Heal</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/skills/heal) check or magical healing), then the character no longer loses [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points). The character can make a DC 10 [<u>Constitution</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores#TOC-Constitution-Con-) check each hour to become conscious and [<u>disabled</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Disabled) (even though his [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points) are still negative). The character takes a penalty on this roll equal to his negative hit point total.

If a character has become stable on his own and hasn’t had help, he is still at risk of losing [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points). Each hour he can make a [<u>Constitution</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores#TOC-Constitution-Con-) check to become stable (as a character that has received aid), but each failed check causes him to lose 1 hit point.

##### Staggered

A staggered creature may take a single [<u>move action</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Move-Actions) or [<u>standard action</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Standard-Actions) each round (but not both, nor can he take full-round actions). A staggered creature can still take free, swift, and immediate actions. A creature with [<u>nonlethal damage</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Nonlethal-Damage) exactly equal to its current [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points) gains the staggered condition.

##### Stunned 1\|2

A stunned creature drops everything held, can’t take actions, takes a –2 penalty to [<u>AC</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Armor-Class), and loses its [<u>Dexterity</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores#TOC-Dexterity-Dex-) bonus to [<u>AC</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Armor-Class) (if any).

Attackers receive a +4 bonus on attack rolls to perform [<u>combat maneuvers</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Combat-Maneuvers) against a stunned opponent.

##### Unconscious

Unconscious creatures are knocked out and [<u>helpless</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/Conditions/#TOC-Helpless). Unconsciousness can result from having negative [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points) (but not more than the creature’s [<u>Constitution</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/basics-ability-scores/ability-scores#TOC-Constitution-Con-) score), or from [<u>nonlethal damage</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Nonlethal-Damage) in excess of current [<u>hit points</u>](https://www.d20pfsrd.com/gamemastering/combat#TOC-Hit-Points).
